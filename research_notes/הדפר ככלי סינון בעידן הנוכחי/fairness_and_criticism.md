# Criticisms, fairness issues and real-world consequences of the IDF Dapar (דפ"ר – דירוג פסיכוטכני ראשוני)

Research date: 2026-11-23. Each finding carries its own date. Anything before 2021 (the October 2021 reform) is marked HISTORICAL. Prep-course company sites are marked [COMMERCIAL]. Opinion columns are marked [OP-ED].

Background needed to read the findings: the Dapar is a computerized adaptive cognitive test. It is scored 10–90 in steps of 10, with a mean of 50 and an SD of about 20 (rounded), and the IDF and Israel Police both use it — [Hebrew Wikipedia, "דירוג פסיכוטכני ראשוני"](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99). Until October 2021 it fed into the קב"א (quality group, 41–56) composite. That composite was officially abolished in the 2021 reform — [Jerusalem Post, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/idf-changing-the-way-it-recruits-soldiers-676422).

## 1. Group differences and cultural/linguistic bias (Ethiopian-Israelis, immigrants, Arabs/Druze/Bedouin/Circassians, Haredim, periphery, SES); what official bodies said; alternative tests and norms

### Takeaway
There is official evidence of large score and placement gaps for Ethiopian-Israelis and for periphery and low-SES youth. Knesset hearings, a Brookdale (JDC) study, State Comptroller follow-ups and IDF officials all document them. The IDF answered mainly by adjusting thresholds and quotas, letting candidates test in their own language, and, in 2026, building a dedicated Haredi version. It did not publicly re-norm or replace the core test. No public psychometric study shows statistical (predictive) bias in the Dapar. The cultural-bias charge rests on gap data, practitioners' testimony and sociological analysis (Lerer 2021).

### Cited Findings

**Ethiopian-Israelis**
- HISTORICAL (data 2011–2015, published March 2018): 52% of Ethiopian-origin boys enlisted with a kaba below 47, against 23% of all boys enlisting. 89% of Ethiopian-origin boys enlist, against 74% of other Israeli-born boys. 24% of them dropped out of service in 2014 (15% of others), and 53% were jailed at least once during service in 2010 (25% of all male soldiers). 8% reached junior command (14% of all soldiers) and 2% became officers (7%) — [Myers-JDC-Brookdale, Levi, Reznikovski-Kuras & Kahan-Strawczynski, "מענים להכנה לשירות צבאי עבור בני נוער יוצאי אתיופיה", RR-769-18, March 2018](https://brookdale-web.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/2018/04/Heb_Report_769_18.pdf)
- The same Brookdale report says that some managers of prep programs believe the screening tests should have been adapted for Ethiopian-Israelis, "since they come from a different culture, background and conceptual world," so the existing test does not show their abilities. The result, they say, is low kaba and Dapar scores that shape the roles offered. The report also records a concern that a low kaba damages self-image and labels the youth — [Brookdale RR-769-18, 2018](https://brookdale-web.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/2018/04/Heb_Report_769_18.pdf)
- HISTORICAL (1 May 2014): fewer than 2% of Ethiopian-Israelis tested in the highest kaba bracket, against nearly 26% of the general population. Maj. Hila Halpern, who headed the IDF department for soldiers of Ethiopian heritage, called the standardized exams "Western" and an inappropriate measure even for Israeli-born members of the community: they "do not reflect the qualities these kids have." — [Times of Israel, Mitch Ginsburg, 1 May 2014](https://www.timesofisrael.com/battling-to-integrate-the-idfs-misunderstood-ethiopian-recruits/)
- HISTORICAL (22 May 2018, Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee, protocol 247). The Manpower Directorate representative, Eran Sheni, described the men's kaba as roughly 50% Dapar, 30% interview and 20% years of schooling, and said Hebrew makes up about 25% of the Dapar. For men the officer "core" pool normally starts at Dapar 60. The IDF did not change the test, but it counts Ethiopian-origin candidates in the officer core from Dapar 30 upward. The minimum kaba for a special-forces selection day is 51, but Ethiopian-origin candidates are admitted at 49. In the same session the Justice Ministry's anti-racism coordinator, Avka Zana, called the assessment method flawed and discriminatory and asked for a systemic review. MK Elazar Stern said the kaba reflects a narrow standard. MK Anat Berko warned that adding more tests creates test-prep advantages. CAVEAT: the source is a machine-generated transcript on Open Knesset, so figures should be checked against the official protocol — [Open Knesset, FADC meeting 22 May 2018](https://oknesset.org/meetings/2/0/2069813.html)
- HISTORICAL (same 2018 hearing): the IDF reported that the share of Ethiopian-origin women with kaba 51+ rose from about 12% to 17.6%, still below the IDF average. It also said Ethiopian-origin entrants to officer courses rose from 64 (2014) to 116 (2017). MK Pnina Tamano-Shata disputed that the percentage had meaningfully changed — [Open Knesset, 22 May 2018](https://oknesset.org/meetings/2/0/2069813.html)
- HISTORICAL (c. 2012): Ethiopian-Israelis were about 1.1% of IDF officers despite enlisting at above-population rates. This comes from a search summary of the ynet article and was not fetched in full — [ynet, "למה יש רק סא"ל אחד שעלה מאתיופיה?"](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4266071,00.html)

**Palmor Committee (July 2016)**
- The government team to eradicate racism against Ethiopian-Israelis was set up by government decision 1107 of 4 Feb 2016 and headed by Justice Ministry DG Emi Palmor. Its IDF chapter does not single out the Dapar. It records the IDF's own plan for the "screening and identification" stage, which includes "relevant adaptations in the screening tools and integration of multicultural components" and "setting identification quotas for officer tracks and quality destinations to ensure broader representation." It also describes "changing the officer-selection model to identify broad potential among Ethiopian-Israelis" and prep for screening tests "with emphasis on the pre-conscript stage." The segregated Amir prep course was to be replaced by an integrated "Kelim" course under the principle of "integration, not separation" — [Palmor team final report, July 2016, pp. 104–105](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/law/25_ls_bk_4169558.pdf)
- The Palmor report also recommends, for civil-service tenders, "examining and adapting entrance tests for candidates from different and diverse backgrounds." That recommendation concerns civil-service exams, not the Dapar directly — [Palmor report, 2016](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/law/25_ls_bk_4169558.pdf)
- HISTORICAL (2014): segregated IDF courses for Ethiopian-Israelis such as Amir, which assessed learning potential using the Feuerstein method, let the IDF place soldiers in air-force and intelligence roles that their test results would have closed. The courses were also widely denounced. MK Tamano-Shata called Amir "an embarrassment and a disgrace and a certificate of poverty" — [Times of Israel, 1 May 2014](https://www.timesofisrael.com/battling-to-integrate-the-idfs-misunderstood-ethiopian-recruits/)

**New immigrants and ethnic origin (historical cultural bias)**
- Sociologist Dr. Ze'ev Lerer (Open University) argues in *הקוד האתני: קב"א, מזרחים, אשכנזים* (Van Leer / HaKibbutz HaMeuchad, 2021; ch. 2, "הדפ"ר וההטייה התרבותית", pp. 54–57) that the Dapar was culturally biased despite claiming objectivity. He reports score differences between immigrants from different countries of origin, and says these gaps shrank or disappeared when candidates were retested after an adjustment period in the army — [Hebrew Wikipedia, Dapar entry (summarizing Lerer)](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99); [Simania book record](https://simania.co.il/bookdetails.php?item_id=1001275)
- According to the Wikipedia entry on the kaba, which relies on Lerer, Dapar questions gave graduates of academic high schools an advantage over everyone else, and the IDF's approach "perpetuated and even exacerbated cultural gaps" — [Hebrew Wikipedia, "מדד קב"א"](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%93_%D7%A7%D7%91%22%D7%90)
- Lerer attributes the historical bias to "bureaucratic ignorance by the manpower officials," not intentional discrimination (quoted 13 Apr 2023) — [Globes English, Assaf Gilead, 13 Apr 2023](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-are-the-israeli-armys-technology-units-elitist-1001443862)
- Language accommodation (official, Aug 2021): from October 2021, recruits can take the test in their language of choice, and Hebrew testing became remote speaking and reading-comprehension tests. Passing the Hebrew test is no longer required for command roles — [Jerusalem Post, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/idf-changing-the-way-it-recruits-soldiers-676422)
- Unofficial [COMMERCIAL] sources say the translated Dapar is offered in Russian, Amharic, English, French, Spanish and Arabic, and that the score is not affected by the test language. The official practice module (לומדה) is reported to be in Hebrew, English and Russian — [dapar.co.il, "עולה חדש בצו הראשון"](https://dapar.co.il/%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%94-%D7%97%D7%93%D7%A9-%D7%A6%D7%95-%D7%94%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F/); [adrenalin-israel.co.il, צו ראשון guide](https://adrenalin-israel.co.il/%D7%A6%D7%95-%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F/)
- HISTORICAL (2018): the IDF says it corrects for language bias when a candidate's Hebrew scores are notably weaker than the rest — [Open Knesset, 22 May 2018](https://oknesset.org/meetings/2/0/2069813.html)

**Haredim**
- 2026: the IDF plans a dedicated Dapar for yeshiva students, built with ממד"א (the Behavioral Sciences Center). It is meant to measure learning potential rather than prior knowledge of core subjects (לימודי ליבה). Manpower Directorate officials are quoted: "Because of the gaps, we built questions that check who is in front of us and what their potential is," and "A Haredi soldier who might have failed a regular test is found suitable despite his training gaps." No launch date or score data was published. The article was last updated 3 Feb 2026 — [Walla, Amir Bohbot](https://www.walla.co.il/news/military/3813866)
- A ynet piece (search snippet; date not verified) likewise says unique tests will be built for soldiers educated in yeshivas without full core studies — [ynet, "התוכנית המיוחדת בצבא לקליטת חרדים"](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/yokra13854784)
- Context: the State Comptroller found that 84% of Haredi boys in secondary education attended institutions with no core studies at all, as reported by Calcalist (date not verified) — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local/articles/0,7340,L-3815990,00.html)

**Periphery and SES**
- Aug 2021: the official rationale for scrapping the kaba included criticism that it was "distorted and irrelevant," with "archaic components that hurt youth from the periphery and lower socioeconomic backgrounds" — [ynet, "הסוף לקב"א ולדפ"ר בצה"ל", Aug 2021](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1c3czwlk)
- HISTORICAL (6 Dec 2018) [OP-ED]: published screening data show that youth from affluent areas receive "quality" placements more often than youth from poorer peripheral areas. The column gives no specific numbers — [ynet, Shmuel Ezra, "כבר בצו ראשון לצה"ל הפריפריה נשארת מאחור", 6 Dec 2018](https://ynet.co.il/news/article/5420559)
- (Placement and representation outcomes by SES and periphery are in Section 5.)

**Arabs, Druze, Bedouin, Circassians**
- I found no published data comparing Dapar scores for Druze, Circassian, Bedouin or Arab-Christian conscripts. An IDF historical article on Druze integration says the Minorities Unit was considered vital partly because it helped recruits overcome gaps, including insufficient Hebrew — [IDF, "שילוב הדרוזים בצה"ל: שלושים השנים הראשונות", Ma'arachot/Yesodot](https://www.idf.il/media/v3rh4hbo/yesodot-4-a-18-a.pdf)
- A 2023 ynet report on the State Comptroller says Druze-sector schools are peripheral but invest heavily in education and perform well. It offers this as a counterexample to a simple periphery-equals-low-scores story — [ynet, Feb/Mar 2023](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1id5e30i)

### Inferences
- The IDF's main remedy for group gaps up to 2018 was to keep the test and lower thresholds for specific groups: Dapar 30 instead of 60 for the officer core, and kaba 49 instead of 51 for special-forces selection. The approach implicitly treats the score as under-predicting these groups' potential, which is an implicit acknowledgement of bias. It also stigmatizes, because the candidates are admitted "below the bar."
- Lerer's finding that gaps closed on retest after time in the army fits a familiarity or acculturation effect rather than fixed ability differences. It also supports the 2021 move to allow retakes.
- The 2026 Haredi version is the first documented case of a separate Dapar instrument for a population group. Ethiopian-Israelis and Arabic speakers got thresholds, quotas and translation, not a separate test.

### Gaps
- No published differential-prediction or DIF (differential item functioning) study of the Dapar by group, or any IDF validity data, was found. ממד"א research is generally unpublished.
- No current (2021–2026) data on Dapar score distributions by ethnicity, immigration cohort, Arab/Druze/Bedouin/Circassian origin or SES cluster was found.
- No Knesset Research and Information Center (ממ"מ) document focused on the Dapar was found. The one MMM recruitment document retrieved (6 Jan 2025, on recruitment arrangements by population group) does not discuss the Dapar.
- Whether the Palmor-era threshold adjustments for Ethiopian-Israelis survived the 2021 reform and the abolition of the kaba is undocumented.
- No source was found for a non-verbal Dapar version or for separate norms (as opposed to adjusted cut-offs).

## 2. The test-prep industry and coaching effects

### Takeaway
A paid prep industry for צו ראשון has existed since at least the mid-2010s. In 2016 it charged about 600–1,200 NIS per candidate and served "a few thousand" candidates a year. The IDF's line moved from "you cannot prepare for a reasoning test" (2016) to publishing official sample questionnaires and an adaptive practice module (from 2021), partly to neutralize the paid advantage. Research on analogous tests suggests coaching raises scores by about 0.25 SD, which is modest but meaningful on a 10-point-step scale. Prep companies' claims of 20-point or larger gains are unverified.

### Cited Findings
- HISTORICAL (14 Sep 2016): "dozens" of institutes, plus private tutors, sold צו ראשון prep, with an estimated few thousand customers a year and no precise figure. Prices: Noam 824 NIS (700 without the kit), Hadas 690 NIS full or 240 NIS online, Hadar 1,200 NIS; home kits 350–600 NIS. A Noam representative said "yes" when asked whether prep would raise a candidate from kaba 52 to 56. Hadar admitted it had no research showing prep raises scores — [mako, "תעשיית ההכנה למבחנים הפסיכוטכניים: כמה עולה תפקיד טוב בצה"ל", 14 Sep 2016](https://www.mako.co.il/pzm-weekend/Article-eba572815492751006.htm)
- IDF position, 2016 (HISTORICAL): Lt. Col. Nimrod Erzuan, deputy commander of מיטב, said "a thinking-skills test is not something you can prepare for" and that "the evaluators know how to identify candidates who went through preparation." He called prep a "false representation" (מצג שווא) and warned that candidates placed in roles that don't suit them will fail. He pointed to free official practice tests on the IDF website. The article asks whether good roles should go to "whoever pays the most" — [mako, 14 Sep 2016](https://www.mako.co.il/pzm-weekend/Article-eba572815492751006.htm)
- HISTORICAL (6 Dec 2018) [OP-ED; author runs a non-profit immigrant prep program at Aluma]: the IDF, the Education Ministry and the Defense Ministry "maintain that the screening tests are like IQ tests and cannot be prepared for." The author argues every section can be prepared for, and says candidates in his program gained on average about 20 points (on the 10–90 scale) between their first and last practice tests. No sample size or methodology is given, and the figure compares practice tests, not real Dapar scores. He also says paid courses are used mainly by families who can afford them, which widens the gaps — [ynet, Shmuel Ezra, 6 Dec 2018](https://ynet.co.il/news/article/5420559)
- Aug 2021: before the reform, "many candidates paid thousands of shekels to private institutes" for צו ראשון prep. As part of the reform the IDF bought a bank of 1,000 new items for about 300,000 NIS and began sending sample questionnaires to candidates' phones through the Meitav app — [ynet, Aug 2021](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1c3czwlk)
- Since October 2021, sample questionnaires have been available on the Meitav site, which reverses the earlier "no preparation" stance — [Hebrew Wikipedia, Dapar entry](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F%D7%99). The official site hosts an adaptive Dapar practice module (לומדת דפ"ר אדפטיבי), described as meant to "prepare you in the best way for the real test" — [IDF Mitgaisim, לומדת דפ"ר אדפטיבי](https://www.mitgaisim.idf.il/%D7%9B%D7%AA%D7%91%D7%95%D7%AA/%D7%9B%D7%AA%D7%91%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%A8-%D7%94%D7%96%D7%9E%D7%9F/%D7%A6%D7%95-%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F/%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%AA-%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%A8-%D7%90%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%98%D7%99%D7%91%D7%99/)
- 13 Apr 2023: subsidized prep courses are now available as part of the effort to widen periphery access — [Globes English, 13 Apr 2023](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-are-the-israeli-armys-technology-units-elitist-1001443862)
- 2018: the Education Ministry told the Knesset that schools would run a coordinated צו ראשון prep program for all students "starting next year" (2019). Brookdale lists "preparation for צו ראשון in schools in 11th grade, for all students in those classes" as one of the answers available — [Open Knesset, 22 May 2018](https://oknesset.org/meetings/2/0/2069813.html); [Brookdale RR-769-18](https://brookdale-web.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/2018/04/Heb_Report_769_18.pdf)
- Current industry [COMMERCIAL], 2025–2026: providers include Machon Noam (adaptive kit with 5 full simulations), Pazatsta (retest-specific packages "developed by veterans of the IDF assessment system and ממד"א"), Adrenalin (צו ראשון track at 149 NIS), Kidum and others. Noam claims the free official module "is not really enough", which is a marketing claim — [machon-noam.co.il](https://www.machon-noam.co.il/%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99-%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%A8); [pazatsta.co.il](https://pazatsta.co.il/%D7%A6%D7%95-%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F/dapar-retest/); [adrenalin-israel.co.il](https://adrenalin-israel.co.il/%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%A8/)
- Research on coaching (Israel): in a randomized study of the Israeli Psychometric Entrance Test (PET), coaching raised scores by about 0.25 SD and did not reduce predictive validity or create prediction bias against uncoached candidates — [Allalouf & Ben-Shakhar, *Journal of Educational Measurement* 35(1):31–47, 1998 (NITE abstract)](https://www.nite.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/e218_abstract.pdf); [PDF](https://www.openu.ac.il/personal_sites/gershon-ben-shakhar/A&BJEM98.pdf)
- Research on retesting and coaching (international): a meta-analysis of cognitive-ability tests in selection (50 studies, 107 samples, about 134,000 people) found an adjusted retest effect of d ≈ 0.26, with larger gains when candidates were coached and when identical forms were reused — [Hausknecht et al., *Journal of Applied Psychology* 92:373–385, 2007](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/4fbba765-0d9c-4940-8492-66fecaca5227/content). A later meta-analysis of 122 studies found that retest interval and age moderate the effect — [Scharfen, Peters & Holling, 2018](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/iq/2018-scharfen.pdf)

### Inferences
- With the Dapar's SD of about 20, a 0.25 SD coaching effect is about 5 points, half a 10-point step. That can be decisive near cut-offs such as Dapar 60 or 70 for officer and technology pipelines. The roughly 20-point gain claimed in the 2018 op-ed and by prep marketing is far above the research literature and should be treated as unverified.
- Allalouf and Ben-Shakhar's result (validity is preserved under coaching) means coaching probably does not make the test less predictive. It does make access to points depend on money, which is a fairness problem rather than a validity problem. The IDF's 2021 response of free official practice, an item bank, subsidized courses and retakes follows from this logic.
- Adaptive testing and a large item bank reduce item exposure and memorization, but they do not remove familiarity effects with item types.

### Gaps
- No IDF or ממד"א data on actual Dapar gains from coaching or retakes was found, and no study specific to the Dapar was found.
- No current (2021–2026) estimate of the prep market's size or revenue was found.
- No study on Raven-type matrices specifically within the Dapar was found. The general literature on matrices practice effects was not retrieved in detail.

## 3. Test-taking conditions: age, anxiety, ADHD and learning disabilities, fatigue, language, single sitting

### Takeaway
Candidates take the Dapar at about 16.5–17 in a single high-stakes sitting. Before 2021 the צו ראשון day lasted about seven hours, with no retake. The State Comptroller documented poor physical testing conditions in 2016. Extra time for learning disabilities and ADHD has existed since at least 2002, but it requires pre-approved professional documentation, which is a barrier for families without access to private diagnosis. The 2021 reform shortened the day and added retakes and remote options.

### Cited Findings
- [OP-ED] (2018): the screening at about age 16.5 sets data that follow a recruit through service — [ynet, Ezra, 6 Dec 2018](https://ynet.co.il/news/article/5420559)
- HISTORICAL (State Comptroller annual report 67A, 2016, p. 1135): computers used in quality screening were often faulty or unsuitable, and many screens were dirty, scribbled on or scratched, which hindered testing. Most psychotechnical units in recruitment offices were overcrowded and short of testing stations — [Hebrew Wikipedia, Dapar entry, citing the Comptroller](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99)
- Fatigue and length: the IDF said shortening צו ראשון was meant to "prevent the burnout of the recruits and provide them with a sense of fairness." JPost reported a cut from about 7 to about 5 hours — [Jerusalem Post, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/idf-changing-the-way-it-recruits-soldiers-676422). ynet and Wikipedia report 7 to 4 hours for tests plus interview — [ynet, Aug 2021](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1c3czwlk). (The sources conflict: 5 vs 4 hours.)
- Accommodations, HISTORICAL baseline: candidates with learning disabilities are entitled to extended time, per the State Comptroller's 2002 report on screening and placement (annual report 53A, pp. 129–130) — [Hebrew Wikipedia, Dapar entry](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99)
- Current accommodation procedure [COMMERCIAL, unverified against official text]: an adapted test requires a summary from a ועדת אפיון וזכאות, a full diagnosis, or an ADHD medication prescription or diagnosis signed by an authorized professional (a school counselor's letter is not enough). Documents must be uploaded to Mitgaisim and approved before צו ראשון. Reported adaptations include about 25% extra time per question and breaks for those with an approved diagnosis — [machon-noam.co.il, אבחון לקויות למידה בצו הראשון](https://www.machon-noam.co.il/%D7%A6%D7%95-%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F-%D7%90%D7%91%D7%97%D7%95%D7%9F); [michvan.co.il](https://www.michvan.co.il/pages/10/%D7%94%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%94_%D7%9C%D7%A6%D7%95_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F_%D7%9E%D7%91%D7%97%D7%9F_%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%A8_%D7%94%D7%A7%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%91%D7%A6%D7%95_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F). The IDF's Mitgaisim site has a page on "the changes in the new accommodations in the Dapar test" (cited by Wikipedia; not retrieved).
- Anxiety: in 2016 one prep provider (Hadas) marketed its sessions, run by psychologists, as reducing candidate anxiety, and a former customer said prep reduced his stress — [mako, 14 Sep 2016](https://www.mako.co.il/pzm-weekend/Article-eba572815492751006.htm). Brookdale (2018) found that many Ethiopian-origin pre-conscripts expressed fear of military service and lacked information about the process. Often they are the first in their family to serve, so parents cannot guide them — [Brookdale RR-769-18](https://brookdale-web.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/2018/04/Heb_Report_769_18.pdf)
- Remote and regional access: from 2021 the IDF planned remote screening for periphery recruits and new stations in the north and south — [Jerusalem Post, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/idf-changing-the-way-it-recruits-soldiers-676422). By April 2023 tests could be taken in Beersheba, Tiberias, Jerusalem and Haifa, and two-thirds of tests remotely — [Globes, 13 Apr 2023](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-are-the-israeli-armys-technology-units-elitist-1001443862)
- Post-war [unverified; Israel Hayom article blocked, search summary only]: the Dapar is described as adaptive, "personalized and more challenging," using algorithms/AI, shorter but demanding more focus — [Israel Hayom, "שינוי כיוון בצה"ל: כך נראית ההכנה לצו הראשון בעידן שאחרי המלחמה"](https://www.israelhayom.co.il/military-life/article/19173692)

### Inferences
- Because accommodations depend on a formal private or ministry diagnosis obtained in advance, they probably favor families with resources and knowledge of the system. This mirrors well-documented SES gaps in bagrut accommodations, though no Dapar-specific data confirms it.
- Until 2021 a single bad day was hard to undo: no retake, a 7-hour day, and in 2016 documented poor equipment.

### Gaps
- No data was found on how many candidates receive Dapar accommodations, by SES or region, or on outcomes.
- No Israeli study of test anxiety specific to the Dapar was found.
- The official current accommodation rules (the Mitgaisim page) were not retrieved, and the details above come from commercial sites.

## 4. Retake policy, appeals, and how hard it is to change a low score

### Takeaway
Before October 2021 the Dapar was essentially one-shot: candidates "almost never" got a retake and had no formal appeal. Since the 2021 reform, low scorers can request a retest, and the higher score counts. The threshold has reportedly loosened from Dapar 70 and below to 80 and below, the latter reported as a Mitgaisim policy of August 2026 [COMMERCIAL, unverified]. A higher score does not guarantee a change in placement.

### Cited Findings
- Pre-2021 (HISTORICAL): candidates generally had one chance — [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99). The IDF "previously almost never allowed retakes" — [ynet, Aug 2021](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1c3czwlk)
- October 2021 reform (official): low scorers can retake. School principals' recommendations for retests are accepted. Youth in a service year or a pre-military mechina can use the deferral for re-screening — [ynet, Aug 2021](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1c3czwlk). Recruits may "redo exams if they feel that the grades they receive are not accurate" — [Jerusalem Post, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/idf-changing-the-way-it-recruits-soldiers-676422). Candidates may also appeal the Dapar score and retake it under conditions — [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99)
- By April 2023 candidates "can request a second attempt" — [Globes, 13 Apr 2023](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-are-the-israeli-armys-technology-units-elitist-1001443862)
- Thresholds, conflicting [COMMERCIAL] sources:
  - Older guides say only Dapar 70 and below qualifies, once only. kanlomdim adds that requests are screened strictly — [arielrosenberg.co.il](https://www.arielrosenberg.co.il/blog/dapar-madrich-mlaa); [kanlomdim.co.il](https://www.kanlomdim.co.il/%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%92/%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%9C%D7%A7%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%AA-%D7%9E%D7%91%D7%97%D7%9F-%D7%97%D7%95%D7%96%D7%A8-%D7%9C%D7%A6%D7%95-%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F)
  - Adrenalin says that under a policy Mitgaisim published in August 2026, anyone with Dapar 80 or below can request a retest "without documents," but only after completing all צו ראשון stages and יום המא"ה. The higher of the two scores counts, and a higher score does not guarantee a new or changed placement. An attention-test retest is reportedly available for scores of 3 or below — [adrenalin-israel.co.il, דפר 90 guide](https://adrenalin-israel.co.il/%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%A8/)
  - MySmartArmy (search snippet) says retests became easier from 2026 even at 80, but actual approval odds are low — [mysmartarmy.com](https://mysmartarmy.com/%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%A8-%D7%97%D7%95%D7%96%D7%A8)
  - Noam says the retest itself cannot be appealed and the higher score counts — [machon-noam.co.il](https://www.machon-noam.co.il/%D7%9E%D7%91%D7%97%D7%A0%D7%99-%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%A8-%D7%A6%D7%95-%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F)
  - Another site says the IDF does not allow a direct appeal or a retest for score improvement — [israelonline.co.il](https://www.israelonline.co.il/%D7%A2%D7%9C-%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%A8-80/). The sources contradict each other.
- Police: police candidates may retake after one year — [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99)

### Inferences
- The retake option creates a second fairness layer. Candidates who know to request a retest, and can pay for retest-specific prep (Pazatsta sells two-week and one-month packages that analyze sub-scores from the first test), benefit most. Because the higher score counts, there is no downside to retaking, so informed candidates have a structural incentive the uninformed lack.
- Combined with retest effects of about 0.26 SD (Section 2), a "best-of-two" rule raises expected scores even without real ability change. That may slowly inflate the score distribution among informed candidates.

### Gaps
- The official Mitgaisim retest-policy text (2021–2026) was not retrieved, so the 70 vs 80 threshold and the "August 2026" claim are unverified.
- No data was found on retake volumes, approval rates or average score changes.
- No information was found on any formal appeals body or on court challenges to individual Dapar scores after 2021.

## 5. Consequences: access to elite units, officer, technology and intelligence roles; civilian careers and income; reproduction of inequality; stigma

### Takeaway
Cognitive screening (historically the Dapar and kaba) gates the officer "core" pool and the technology and intelligence pipelines. Those pipelines are in turn a main entry route into Israeli high tech: 30–50% of high-tech workers have an IDF technology background (OECD). The State Comptroller (Feb 2023) and Globes (2023) documented strong over-representation of high-SES and central-region youth in tech units. Critics call military service "the largest gap generator in society." Official programs (Magshimim, Atidim, Nitzanim, Gesharim) have raised periphery shares, from about 16.5% toward about 20% by 2024. Stigma from low scores is documented qualitatively (Brookdale), but the "Dapar 50" social-label phenomenon is not documented in reliable sources.

### Cited Findings
- Gatekeeping (HISTORICAL, 2018): the officer-core pool for men normally starts at Dapar 60 — [Open Knesset, 22 May 2018](https://oknesset.org/meetings/2/0/2069813.html). The kaba, together with other data, set the threshold for invitations to elite units and influenced job assignment and officer-course invitations — [Brookdale RR-769-18, footnote 1](https://brookdale-web.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/2018/04/Heb_Report_769_18.pdf). Low kaba scores led to low-level placements such as drivers and clerks, which the reporting linked to higher dropout — [Times of Israel, 1 May 2014](https://www.timesofisrael.com/battling-to-integrate-the-idfs-misunderstood-ethiopian-recruits/)
- State Comptroller, annual report Feb 2023 (a follow-up audit of a November 2016 report on IDF screening and recruitment): periphery residents serve in the main technological tracks at about half their share of IDF units overall, about 16.5% against about 32%. The deficiency was corrected "to a small extent" — [ynet report on the Comptroller](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1id5e30i); [karmel.co.il summary](https://www.karmel.co.il/%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%AA/%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%A8-%D7%A0%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%9A-%D7%A9%D7%9C-%D7%97%D7%99%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%9E%D7%94%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%94-%D7%91%D7%9E%D7%A1%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%94%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%92%D7%99%D7%99%D7%9D); [State Comptroller annual report, Feb 2023](https://www.mevaker.gov.il/(X(1)S(vt2qdvsz3mqqvfbdpa1jfzkm))/he/publication/Articles/Pages/2023.2-AnnualReport.aspx?AspxAutoDetectCookieSupport=1). Per ynet, residents of socioeconomic deciles 7–9 make up 74% of soldiers in those units. ynet attributes the gap mainly to school quality and parents' ability to pay for tutoring and prep courses, not to discrimination — [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1id5e30i)
- Composition at the end of 2022: the second-highest SES cluster was 37% of tech-unit soldiers against 22% of the population, and the top decile 10% against 4%. Low clusters were far under-represented: 3% against 10%, and 1% against 6%. Periphery residents were 18% of tech-unit soldiers in 2022, with military sources projecting 20–25% by the end of 2023 — [Globes English, 13 Apr 2023](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-are-the-israeli-armys-technology-units-elitist-1001443862)
- 2024 (search summary; article blocked): periphery recruitment to technological tracks rose from 16.5% toward nearly 20%, credited partly to Nitzanim, where about 62% of periphery graduates enter IDF technological roles — [Israel Hayom, "מ-16.5% לכמעט 20%"](https://www.israelhayom.co.il/news/local/article/19978054)
- Labor-market link: 30–50% of all Israeli high-tech workers have an IDF technology-role background, though official data on military background does not exist. Men and advantaged-SES youth are over-represented in technological units — [OECD Economics Dept. Working Paper No. 1787, Dec 2023](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/12/addressing-labour-market-challenges-for-sustainable-and-inclusive-growth-in-israel_99d5312d/dcdb1283-en.pdf)
- Lerer (2023): the "advantages for 8200 graduates in subsequent civilian life are much greater" than for other soldiers — [Globes, 13 Apr 2023](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-are-the-israeli-armys-technology-units-elitist-1001443862)
- A ynet piece (date not shown) says IDF service is an "entry ticket" to high tech, that deciles 7–10 serve in tech units above their population share, and that "military service became the largest gap generator in society" (המחולל הפערים הגדול ביותר בחברה) — [ynet, Yossi Yehoshua, "המסלול לצמצום פערים בהייטק עובר דרך צה"ל"](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/by5xiai9j)
- An OSINT study from 2017, cited by ETH Zurich CSS in December 2019, found that Unit 8200 recruits come disproportionately from the richer, more educated Tel Aviv area and elite high schools, so the unit "misses out on other less fortunate but gifted recruits" — [CSS ETH Zurich, Cyber Report on Unit 8200, Dec 2019](https://css.ethz.ch/content/dam/ethz/special-interest/gess/cis/center-for-securities-studies/pdfs/Cyber-Reports-2019-12-Unit-8200.pdf)
- Earnings evidence is mixed. A study cited by the OECD/Taub search results estimates the average effect of military service on earnings as slightly positive (1–2%) and not significantly different from zero over 2–20 years, with positive effects for less-educated men. The authors were not identified in the retrieved snippet — [OECD WP 1787 / search results](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2023/12/addressing-labour-market-challenges-for-sustainable-and-inclusive-growth-in-israel_99d5312d/dcdb1283-en.pdf). (This concerns service in general, not elite units.)
- High-SES boys tend to aim for elite combat or prestigious technological jobs, according to a study of youth attitudes — [Young (SAGE), "Negotiating the Uniform: Youth Attitudes towards Military Service in Israel"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/1103308818787647). RAND notes that willingness to serve has been correlated with higher SES — [RAND RRA1861-1, Harel & Popper](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RRA1800/RRA1861-1/RAND_RRA1861-1.pdf)
- Stigma and self-image: Brookdale (2018) records the concern that a low kaba "affects the self-image of Ethiopian-Israelis and labels them, causing additional harm" — [Brookdale RR-769-18](https://brookdale-web.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/2018/04/Heb_Report_769_18.pdf). The IDF itself argued in 2016 that misplacing candidates by inflated scores leads to failure that hurts them more — [mako, 14 Sep 2016](https://www.mako.co.il/pzm-weekend/Article-eba572815492751006.htm). Youth Q&A forums show anxiety over "I got 50 in the Dapar" — [stips.co.il thread](https://stips.co.il/ask/16337881/%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%AA%D7%99-50-%D7%91%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%A8) (anecdotal).

### Inferences
- The causal chain runs from cognitive screening scores (shaped by schooling, SES and prep access) to eligibility for officer and technology pipelines, to the 8200 and tech-unit network, to the high-tech labor market. Even if the Dapar is psychometrically valid, it acts as a conversion point where pre-existing educational inequality becomes military placement and then civilian advantage. The Comptroller and ynet attribute the gap mainly to school quality and paid tutoring, not to the test itself, and the IDF's counter-argument rests on the same point: the test measures real current ability, which the education system shapes.
- Increases in the periphery share since 2022 come mainly from pipeline programs that develop skills before testing (Magshimim, Nitzanim) rather than from changes to the Dapar.

### Gaps
- No Israeli study was found that directly links individual Dapar scores to adult earnings or higher-education outcomes.
- No reliable source was found on the "Dapar 50" social label or jokes among youth. Only anecdotal forum posts exist.
- Recent (2023–2026) data on officer-course entry by group, and on Dapar distributions by locality cluster, was not found.

## 6. Ethical and privacy concerns: the score treated as an "IQ score", secrecy, leaks, use by employers and police

### Takeaway
The Dapar was historically secret even from the examinee. Disclosure followed a court ruling, dated 2009 in one source and 2005 in another. Since 2021 candidates receive section-level scores. The IDF and ministries long described the test as IQ-like, which feeds the public "IQ score" framing. Israel Police also uses Dapar thresholds. No reliable evidence was found of employers using Dapar scores or of documented leaks.

### Cited Findings
- Secrecy and disclosure: until 2009 Dapar scores were not disclosed to candidates. A court ruling at the end of 2009 required disclosure (Wikipedia cites Walla, 31 Dec 2009; the original Walla link is dead) — [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99). This is contradicted by an [OP-ED] saying the High Court ordered publication of screening data (kaba, Dapar, profile) in 2005 — [ynet, Ezra, 6 Dec 2018](https://ynet.co.il/news/article/5420559). The date is unresolved.
- From October 2021: near-complete disclosure of results, broken down by section — [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99); [Jerusalem Post, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/idf-changing-the-way-it-recruits-soldiers-676422). Candidates receive an overall Dapar (10–90) and a 1–9 score per section — [IDF Mitgaisim adaptive module page](https://www.mitgaisim.idf.il/%D7%9B%D7%AA%D7%91%D7%95%D7%AA/%D7%9B%D7%AA%D7%91%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%A8-%D7%94%D7%96%D7%9E%D7%9F/%D7%A6%D7%95-%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F/%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%AA-%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%A8-%D7%90%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%98%D7%99%D7%91%D7%99/) (per search summary)
- "IQ" framing: the Dapar is "similar in many respects to IQ tests" — [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99). The IDF, the Education Ministry and the Defense Ministry described the צו ראשון tests as "a kind of IQ test" ([OP-ED, ynet, 6 Dec 2018](https://ynet.co.il/news/article/5420559)). An op-ed calls the Dapar "related to intelligence" — [same](https://ynet.co.il/news/article/5420559)
- Police use: Israel Police uses the Dapar. The minimum rose from 3 to 5 (old 1–9 scale) in the mid-1960s, and a State Comptroller report found many recruits who did not meet the requirement (ynet, 30 Oct 2011) — [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99); [ynet, 30 Oct 2011](https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-4141434,00.html). The police publish an online simulation of their screening tests — [Israel Police](https://www.police.gov.il/join/simulation)

### Inferences
- Moving from total secrecy (before 2009, or 2005) to section-level disclosure (2021) improved transparency and made retakes and appeals meaningful. It may also have intensified the score's role as a social label and the incentive to prep, because candidates now know and compare their numbers.
- The official "IQ-like, can't prepare" framing (2016–2018) and the later official practice materials (2021+) are in tension. The IDF's own reversal undercuts the claim that the score is a fixed measure of innate ability.

### Gaps
- No reliable source was found on Dapar leaks (item-bank leaks or data breaches).
- No reliable source was found on employers or other civilian bodies requesting or using Dapar scores, or on the legal status of such use under Israeli privacy law.
- The 2009 (or 2005) court ruling's case name and number were not identified.

## 7. IDF responses and reforms (2016–2026), including programs that bypass or reduce the weight of the Dapar

### Takeaway
The IDF's response has three layers. First, group-specific adjustments to thresholds and quotas (Ethiopian-Israelis; documented in 2016–2018). Second, the October 2021 reform under Maj. Gen. Yaniv Asor: the kaba was abolished, the Dapar made adaptive, shorter and re-takeable, with language choice, section-level disclosure, free practice materials, remote and regional testing, and a multi-factor "fitness score." Third, upstream pipeline programs that develop periphery talent before screening (Magshimim, Atidim, Nitzanim, Gesharim). In 2025–2026 it added parallel screening and placement from 11th grade for non-combat roles and a dedicated Haredi Dapar. The IDF's consistent counter-argument is that a standardized cognitive measure is needed for operational effectiveness, and that gaps originate in the education system.

### Cited Findings
- 2016 (Palmor, IDF chapter): the IDF's Ethiopian-Israeli integration plan lists screening-tool adaptations and multicultural components, identification quotas for officer and "quality" tracks, a changed officer-selection model, General Staff identification committees for officer candidates, and prep for screening tests. The governing principle is "integration, not separation" — [Palmor report, July 2016, pp. 104–105](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/law/25_ls_bk_4169558.pdf)
- 2013 (HISTORICAL): Maj. Gen. Orna Barbivai, head of the Manpower Directorate, funded a department dedicated to soldiers of Ethiopian heritage — [Times of Israel, 1 May 2014](https://www.timesofisrael.com/battling-to-integrate-the-idfs-misunderstood-ethiopian-recruits/)
- 2018: the IDF told the Knesset it ended the Amir course and Ethiopian-specific officer conferences after a government decision. It described the kaba as a standardized tool to which other instruments are being added "to reduce its narrow influence," mentioned the multi-year overhaul of selection tools (TERASH), and said quotas in pre-military academies would expand. The Ofek mechinot had about 63 Ethiopian-origin students out of 656 — [Open Knesset, 22 May 2018](https://oknesset.org/meetings/2/0/2069813.html)
- August 2021 announcement, in force 1 Oct 2021: kaba abolished and replaced by a role-specific "fitness score" (ציון התאמה). Dapar replaced by expanded, partly adaptive tests; calculators allowed; day shortened; scores disclosed by section; retakes allowed; Hebrew test made remote and no longer a bar to command; testing in a language of choice. יום המא"ה (previously women only) opened to men. Local-authority recruitment programs were to grow from a 30-authority pilot to 100 by the end of 2021, then all 132 — [Jerusalem Post, 11 Aug 2021](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/idf-changing-the-way-it-recruits-soldiers-676422). Maj. Gen. Yaniv Asor is named as the architect. Within two years the system was to add measures of resilience, persistence, working memory, spatial orientation, fine motor skills, teamwork, leadership, social mobility, hobbies, and Education Ministry grades and study tracks, combined by an algorithmic "data blender" trained on soldiers who completed good service. The article says the Dapar and kaba "will not be cancelled entirely" but updated — [ynet, Aug 2021](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1c3czwlk); [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99). The stated goals were placement by suitability for a role rather than by test results, "a sense of fairness," equal opportunity for the periphery, and better use of manpower — [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/idf-changing-the-way-it-recruits-soldiers-676422)
- 2023: other tests were refined and their weight in the final grade reduced. Regional and remote testing and subsidized prep were introduced — [Globes, 13 Apr 2023](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-are-the-israeli-armys-technology-units-elitist-1001443862)
- Pipeline programs:
  - Magshimim was founded under Unit 8200 commander Nadav Zafrir (2009–2013) and is 20% funded by the Rashi Foundation. In 2023, 1,700 completed it, 35% were accepted to tech units, 65% of graduates were recruited by cyber and tech units, and 85% go on to degrees or tech work — [Globes, 13 Apr 2023](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-are-the-israeli-armys-technology-units-elitist-1001443862). It operates in 27 centers, and 74% of periphery youth who reached tech units were Magshimim graduates — [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/yokra14218319). It raised periphery recruits in tech units fivefold — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/article/sjyp1bzc5)
  - Atidim gives periphery youth academic degrees during service and accounts for 28% of soldiers in degree tracks (2023) — [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-are-the-israeli-armys-technology-units-elitist-1001443862); [IDF, 20 years of Atidim, 2018](https://www.idf.il/%D7%90%D7%AA%D7%A8%D7%99-%D7%99%D7%97%D7%99%D7%93%D7%95%D7%AA/%D7%90%D7%92%D7%A3-%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A2%D7%99%D7%9F/%D7%9B%D7%9C-%D7%94%D7%9B%D7%AA%D7%91%D7%95%D7%AA/2018/%D7%AA%D7%95%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%AA-%D7%A2%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%93%D7%99%D7%9D/)
  - Gesharim (Bridges): 8200-trained women soldiers teach programming in grades 7–9 in 34–35 periphery towns. The army funds 50%. 24,000 students in 2023, with a target of 50,000 by 2025 — [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-are-the-israeli-armys-technology-units-elitist-1001443862); [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1id5e30i)
  - Nitzanim (run by בנתיבי אודי with the IDF and Education Ministry): about 50% of periphery youth who entered IDF computing clusters in the 2024 cohort were Nitzanim graduates — [Ice](https://www.ice.co.il/digital-140/news/article/1051593)
- Dec 2025: for non-combat roles, screening and placement now run in parallel. The IDF reviews personal data, screening results and preferences from 11th grade, so candidates for selective tracks (such as the Navy or Intelligence) know their fallback placement in advance. Combat-designated men are excluded. The Manpower Directorate's stated aim is earlier certainty and "operational effectiveness" — [Maariv, Avi Ashkenazi, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.maariv.co.il/news/military/article-1262638)
- 2026: a dedicated Dapar for Haredi yeshiva students (see Section 1), alongside a Haredi recruitment unit within מיטב and adjusted interview questions — [Walla, updated 3 Feb 2026](https://www.walla.co.il/news/military/3813866)
- The IDF's counter-arguments:
  - Standardization and validity: the kaba is "a standardized tool" (2018) — [Open Knesset](https://oknesset.org/meetings/2/0/2069813.html).
  - Prepped candidates misplaced in roles that don't suit them will fail (2016) — [mako](https://www.mako.co.il/pzm-weekend/Article-eba572815492751006.htm).
  - The new tests have more formal item components, which research reportedly links to less bias — [ynet, Aug 2021](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1c3czwlk).
  - Gaps stem from schools and resources rather than from the screening itself (Comptroller/ynet framing, 2023) — [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1id5e30i).

### Inferences
- The 2021 reform answered most of the documented criticisms on paper: one-shot testing, secrecy, length, language, the prep advantage and the periphery's physical access. But the Dapar, adaptive and still central, remains the cognitive core of the new score.
- Since 2022 the largest measurable gains in fairness of outcomes (periphery shares in tech units) come from pre-test pipeline programs rather than from the test. In effect the IDF accepted that the Dapar measures developed ability and moved to develop the ability earlier.

### Gaps
- No public IDF evaluation of the 2021 reform's effect on group gaps was found.
- The actual current weight of the Dapar inside the post-2021 "fitness score" is undisclosed.
- No statement by Manpower Directorate heads (Asor, or Maj. Gen. Dado Bar-Kalifa, appointed Nov 2024 per Hebrew Wikipedia) specifically on Dapar fairness was found in 2022–2026 sources.

## 8. Effects of the 2023–2025 war period on screening and placement

### Takeaway
The war created severe manpower shortages: about 10,000–12,000 soldiers, including 6,000–7,500 combat troops. The documented screening changes are medical-profile and process changes: a new Profile 70, parallel screening and placement, and Haredi-specific tracks. No documented change to Dapar cognitive thresholds was found.

### Cited Findings
- Shortages: the IDF told the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee of a shortage of about 12,000 soldiers, of them 6,000–7,000 combat troops (search summary). The head of the Manpower Directorate, Maj. Gen. Dado Bar-Kalifa, put the need at about 10,000, including 7,000 combat troops. The number of youth receiving profile 21 (exemption) fell from about 10,000 in 2023 to about 8,200 in 2025 — [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/bjbhxvv1fg); [Walla](https://www.walla.co.il/news/military/3838808); [Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/427211)
- Profile 70, date not shown, roughly 2025–2026: a new medical profile between 64 and 72 lets recruits who were previously rear-echelon-only be screened for combat units (not infantry). The IDF Spokesperson: "This move will make it possible to expand the ranks of those serving." It is to be applied at the צו ראשון stage — [ynetnews, Gal Ganot](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/hyuj61200mg); [Israel National News, "Profile 70"](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/432784)
- 15 Dec 2025: parallel screening and placement from 11th grade for non-combat roles (see Section 7) — [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/news/military/article-1262638)
- 2026: a dedicated Haredi Dapar and a Haredi recruitment unit, linked to expanded Haredi draft efforts — [Walla, 3 Feb 2026](https://www.walla.co.il/news/military/3813866)

### Inferences
- Under shortage pressure, the IDF widened eligibility mainly on the medical dimension and on Haredi inclusion, not by lowering Dapar cut-offs. The Haredi Dapar is effectively the first population-specific cognitive instrument, driven by manpower need as much as by fairness.

### Gaps
- No source documents any change to Dapar thresholds for technology, intelligence or officer roles during or after the war.
- No data was found on how the war affected צו ראשון scheduling, remote testing, retake volumes or prep-market demand.
