# The IDF Dapar (דפ"ר): construct coverage, reliability and validity

Research notes compiled 23 Nov 2026. Scope: what the Dapar measures and does not measure, and the published evidence on its psychometric quality. Important caveat for the report writer: almost all Dapar-specific psychometric numbers in the open literature are cited second-hand from **Gal (1986), *A Portrait of the Israeli Soldier*** and from **unpublished IDF reports (Reeb 1968/1969/1976)**. I found no peer-reviewed psychometric technical report on the current computerized adaptive Dapar. Numbers labelled "IDF internal" cannot be checked independently.

---

## 1. What construct does the Dapar measure? (g/CHC mapping; correlations with IQ, the psychometric exam and Bagrut)

### Takeaway
The Dapar is a short group intelligence battery with four subtests: Otis-type verbal instructions, Similarities, Arithmetic reasoning, and Raven-type matrices. It is reported on a 9-step stanine-like scale (10–90). It is designed as a measure of general mental ability (g), and its total is said to correlate above .90 with WAIS Full-Scale IQ. That figure comes from IDF internal validation (via Gal 1986) and has not been independently replicated. I found no published correlation between the Dapar and the NITE psychometric exam or Bagrut scores.

### Cited Findings
- **Four subtests (classic battery):** (1) a modified Otis-type "verbal instruction comprehension" test adapted from the U.S. Army Alpha Instructions test; (2) "verbal abstraction and categorization", i.e. Similarities–Differences adapted from WAIS Similarities; (3) "mathematical reasoning", described as "a more challenging version of the WAIS Arithmetic subtest"; (4) "nonverbal abstract reasoning", "similar to Raven's Progressive Matrices". "The sum of these subscale scores yields a validated measure of general intelligence." — [Zalmenson et al., 2026, *Development and Psychopathology*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/development-and-psychopathology/article/when-resilience-breaks-down-erosion-of-the-protective-role-of-intelligence-in-ptsd-under-extreme-combat-stress/094B9101FA5992A5F74D7A95F2916FB1) (citing Gal 1986; Rabinowitz et al. 2000; Weiser et al. 2007, 2017)
- **Historical item formats (1971–1984):** The Matrices test was a Raven adaptation with clusters of rising difficulty and a raw score range of 4–35. The Instructions test had 21 open-ended items, for example "write the last letter of the word which is the opposite of black". "Both tests are timed, and most subjects do not complete either. There is no penalty for wrong answers." Protocol: Matrices, then a Hebrew test; only those who passed Hebrew took Instructions. — [Flynn, 1998, *J. Biosocial Science* 30:541–553 (full text)](http://www.iapsych.com/iqmr/fe/LinkedDocuments/flynn1988b.pdf)
- **Coverage:** In 1971–1984 "virtually all males physically present in Israel" at age 17.5 were tested, about 28,000 per year; female testing coverage was roughly 79–80% of male coverage, mainly because religious exemptions were not tested. — [Flynn 1998](http://www.iapsych.com/iqmr/fe/LinkedDocuments/flynn1988b.pdf)
- **Correlation with WAIS:** "Correlations between the total score and WAIS full-scale IQ exceeding 0.90"; "scores at the 95th percentile correspond to an IQ above 135" — [Zalmenson et al. 2026](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/development-and-psychopathology/article/when-resilience-breaks-down-erosion-of-the-protective-role-of-intelligence-in-ptsd-under-extreme-combat-stress/094B9101FA5992A5F74D7A95F2916FB1). Also "a highly valid measure of general intelligence as measured by the WAIS total score (r > 0.90)", citing Gal (1986) — [Rotstein, Goldenberg, Fund, Levine & Reichenberg, 2021, *European Psychiatry* 64(1):e76](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/capturing-adolescents-in-need-of-psychiatric-care-with-psychopathological-symptoms-a-populationbased-cohort-study/8DD8499890537735E32DCCD6E494A0ED)
- **Scale:** Scores are reported as 10–90 in steps of 10, nine levels (historically 1–9), with mean 50 and SD about 20. Historical bands: low 1–3, medium 4–6, high 7–9. Scores below 3 meant no enlistment. Levels 5 and 6 were split into A and B sub-levels for officer selection. — [Hebrew Wikipedia: דירוג פסיכוטכני ראשוני](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99). An older peer-reviewed source likewise describes the "Cognitive Ability Score (DAPAR)" as built from four subtests and reported in **stanines** — [Differential test validity for ethnic groups in the Israel army and the effects of educational level (ResearchGate record)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232494444_Differential_test_validity_for_ethnic_groups_in_the_Israel_army_and_the_effects_of_educational_level)
- **Computerized and adaptive:** Computerized versions were introduced in the 2000s. The test is adaptive, with item difficulty adjusting after each answer, and is described as resembling an IQ test "in many respects". — [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99). Test-prep sites in 2026 describe it as a computerized test of about 2.5 hours (test-prep claim, unverified) — [kosherkravi.co.il](https://kosherkravi.co.il/blog_article/%D7%9E%D7%91%D7%97%D7%9F-%D7%93%D7%A4%D7%A8/)
- **Score distribution in a selected sample:** Among 10,898 candidates for military command positions, the Initial Psychotechnical Rating had mean 56.6 and SD 18.0 — [Avni & Luria, 2025, *EJWOP* 34(3)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1359432X.2025.2457780) (via search abstract; full text paywalled)
- **Raven vs. timed spatial ability:** Flynn argued that the IDF Matrices were so time-pressured that they partly tap spatial visualization and visual memory. He attributed the small female deficit on Matrices (best estimate female IQ 98.6, against 100.8 on the verbal Instructions test) to this. — [Flynn 1998](http://www.iapsych.com/iqmr/fe/LinkedDocuments/flynn1988b.pdf)
- **Comparator: the NITE psychometric exam's own psychometrics.** Reliability: total multi-domain score 0.95; quantitative-weighted and verbal-weighted totals 0.94; domain scores about 0.87–0.92. Predictive validity for academic GPA is mostly in the 0.3–0.5 range ("medium"), with the overall row at about .32–.46 depending on the predictor combination. — [NITE, Kenet-Cohen, "Reliability and validity of the psychometric exam" (2023 version)](https://www.nite.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/rel_val_acc.pdf)

### Inferences
- **CHC mapping (my analysis, from the subtest descriptions above):**
  - Matrices map mainly to **Gf** (fluid reasoning). Because they are timed, they also load on **Gv** and **Gs**, as Flynn argued.
  - Similarities is a classic **Gc** task (verbal concept formation), with some Gf.
  - Arithmetic reasoning is **Gq/RQ** (quantitative reasoning) plus **Gwm**. In WAIS-IV, Arithmetic loads on the Working Memory index.
  - Otis-type Instructions taps **Gc** (language comprehension) plus **Gwm**, attention and **Gs** under time pressure. It closely resembles the Army Alpha and Otis items that were the forerunners of AFQT-type verbal tests.
  - In effect the battery is a compressed "WAIS short form": two verbal subtests (Similarities and Instructions, akin to Otis) and two reasoning subtests (Arithmetic and Raven). Its total is therefore strongly g-loaded.
  - Abilities with thin or no representation: Glr (long-term storage and retrieval), Ga (auditory), Gkn (domain knowledge), Grw (reading/writing beyond Hebrew comprehension), Gv as a separate factor (mental rotation, mechanical reasoning), and psychomotor and perceptual-speed factors.
- **The r > .90 with WAIS is probably an upper-bound figure.** Different full-scale IQ batteries typically correlate about .75–.90 with each other, and a four-subtest group test would be expected near the lower-middle of that range. The figure is undated in the citing articles (Gal 1986 → Reeb reports from the 1960s–70s), may be corrected for unreliability, and predates the computerized adaptive version.
- **The "95th percentile = IQ > 135" claim is internally inconsistent with standard norms.** On a normal curve the 95th percentile is about IQ 125. A stanine-9 cutoff (top 4%) corresponds to about IQ 126. The 135 figure probably reflects an old non-Israeli norm table (Flynn-inflated) or an imprecise translation, and should not be repeated without that caveat.
- **Dapar vs. NITE psychometric vs. Bagrut:** Content overlap is high. Both include quantitative reasoning and verbal analogies and inferences, and NITE's verbal section is Gc-heavy, as is Dapar Similarities. Given the shared g variance, a correlation in the .6–.8 range would be expected. **This is an expectation, not a finding.**

### Gaps
- I found **no published correlation** between the Dapar and (a) the NITE psychometric exam or (b) Bagrut averages. Hebrew and English searches returned only NITE's own Bagrut–psychometric studies. Test-prep sites sometimes assert Dapar–psychometric links but give no data.
- I could not find the item-level structure of the *current* computerized adaptive Dapar from an official source. Test-prep sites describe four parts (verbal analogies, math, following instructions, shape series), which matches the classic battery, but this is unverified.
- No published confirmatory factor analysis or g-loading estimates for Dapar subtests were found.
- Reeb's internal reports (1968, 1969, 1976) and Gal (1986) were not accessible online.

## 2. Reliability: test–retest, internal consistency, SEM, and the coarse 10–90 scale

### Takeaway
The only published reliability numbers are subtest Cronbach's alphas of **0.86–0.91**. These come from **unpublished IDF validation research**, cited in a 2025 peer-reviewed article. I found **no published test–retest coefficient or SEM for the cognitive battery**. The 9-step scale (one step ≈ 0.5 SD ≈ 7.5 IQ points), combined with any realistic SEM, means that a large share of examinees could plausibly be reported one step away from their true band.

### Cited Findings
- **Internal consistency:** "each of the four subtests has a Cronbach's alpha between 0.86 and 0.91", attributed to unpublished IDF validation research — [Avni & Luria 2025, *EJWOP*](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1359432X.2025.2457780) (via search snippet; full text 403)
- **No test–retest figure for the cognitive battery.** Rotstein et al. (2021) and Zalmenson et al. (2026) both report validity but **no reliability coefficient** for the cognitive test — [Rotstein et al. 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/capturing-adolescents-in-need-of-psychiatric-care-with-psychopathological-symptoms-a-populationbased-cohort-study/8DD8499890537735E32DCCD6E494A0ED); [Zalmenson et al. 2026](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/development-and-psychopathology/article/when-resilience-breaks-down-erosion-of-the-protective-role-of-intelligence-in-ptsd-under-extreme-combat-stress/094B9101FA5992A5F74D7A95F2916FB1)
- **Interview reliability (for comparison):** The draft-board behavioural interview has test–retest reliability of ">0.8" when inductees are re-interviewed after several days by different interviewers (Gal 1986; Reeb 1968) — [Rotstein et al. 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/capturing-adolescents-in-need-of-psychiatric-care-with-psychopathological-symptoms-a-populationbased-cohort-study/8DD8499890537735E32DCCD6E494A0ED); the same >0.8 figure is attributed to Reeb (1969) in [Avni & Luria 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1359432X.2025.2457780)
- **Retesting policy, relevant to practice effects:** Until 2021, candidates generally had one attempt. Since October 2021, low scorers may retest and appeal under conditions. Police candidates may retest after one year. Scores have been disclosed to candidates only since a late-2009 court ruling. — [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99)
- **Testing conditions:** A 2016 State Comptroller report criticized dirty, damaged or malfunctioning screens, overcrowding and a shortage of testing stations at recruitment offices (State Comptroller Annual Report 67A, p. 1135) — [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99)
- **Coaching market:** Many candidates paid thousands of shekels to private prep institutes for the first-stage tests, which was one reason given for the 2021 reform — [ynet, Yoav Zitun (2021)](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1c3czwlk)
- **Heaping at cutoffs in the Kaba composite:** Ruffle & Tobol found that Kaba = 52 (the officer-course threshold) was the most frequent score, followed by 49. Near-miss scores of 50–51 were rare (21–22% of the sample). The authors interpret this as the IDF using the subjective components (interview, education score) to "boost to 52" candidates deemed suitable. 48.2% of their N=427 sample were officer-eligible; Lerer reports 48% for 1995. — [Ruffle & Tobol, IZA DP 9860 (2016)](https://cdn2.psychologytoday.com/assets/dp9860.pdf)

### Inferences
- **SEM, my computation.** Inputs: SD = 20 on the 10–90 scale (≈15 IQ points). The composite reliability is unknown, so I use a plausible range of .85–.95; four subtests each with α ≈ .86–.91 would usually give a composite of about .93–.95.
  - SEM is **4.5–7.7 Dapar points**, or **3.4–5.8 IQ points**.
  - The 95% confidence band is **±8.8 to ±15.2 Dapar points**, i.e. about ±1 to ±1.5 reporting steps.
- **Classification error from the 10-point steps, my computation.** Assume normal error and a true score exactly in the *middle* of a 10-point band. The probability that the observed score falls outside that band is about **26% (r=.95), 34% (r=.93), 43% (r=.90), 52% (r=.85)**. For true scores near a band edge, the chance of misclassification approaches 50% by construction.
  - Practical meaning: around any hard cutoff (Dapar ≥50 for officer eligibility; Dapar 90 for elite technology tracks), the score cannot reliably separate adjacent bands for individuals near the line.
  - Rounding to stanine-like steps also discards information: a 10-point step is 0.5 SD, about 7.5 IQ points.
- **Adaptive testing helps.** A CAT can reach high precision with few items, but precision at the extremes depends on item-bank depth. A ceiling at 90 = top ~4% also means the Dapar **cannot discriminate within the top stanine**, the very group from which elite programs (Talpiot, 8200 technology tracks) select.
- **Retesting and coaching:** Allowing retests since 2021 plus a commercial prep market both inflate observed scores for some candidates without changing the underlying ability. This creates construct-irrelevant variance correlated with SES.

### Gaps
- No published test–retest reliability, composite reliability, IRT information function, or SEM for the Dapar, classic or computerized.
- No published data on retest gains (practice/coaching effects) since retesting was allowed in 2021.
- No published decision-consistency or classification-accuracy analysis at cutoffs.

## 3. Predictive validity: military outcomes and later civilian outcomes

### Takeaway
The published evidence is old and thin. The cognitive score correlates about **.40** with general military performance criteria (Reeb 1976, internal) and **>.41 with rank at discharge** (Gal 1986). It shows considerable **differential validity across ethnic subgroups** (N≈7,000). Recent peer-reviewed work uses the Dapar mainly as a predictor of *health* outcomes: lower scores predict steeper PTSD trajectories, a protective effect that eroded under extreme war stress. I found **no publicly available validity coefficients** for Bahad 1, the pilot course or command courses, and **no economics paper linking Dapar or Kaba to wages**.

### Cited Findings
- **General military performance:** A predictive validity of about **r = .40** is cited from Reeb (1976, unpublished IDF report). In a study of about **7,000 male draftees**, the DAPAR showed "considerable differential validity" across Israel-born, Western-born and Eastern-born subgroups, and educational level affected the results. — [Differential test validity for ethnic groups in the Israel army... (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232494444_Differential_test_validity_for_ethnic_groups_in_the_Israel_army_and_the_effects_of_educational_level) (abstract-level information via search; authors and year not retrieved)
- **Rank at discharge:** The cognitive battery correlates **r > 0.41** with rank at discharge (validity data from Gal 1986). The interview's validity for rank after 30 months is **r = 0.39**. — [Rotstein et al. 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/capturing-adolescents-in-need-of-psychiatric-care-with-psychopathological-symptoms-a-populationbased-cohort-study/8DD8499890537735E32DCCD6E494A0ED). Avni & Luria (2025) instead cite **r = 0.32** for the performance/interview rating with rank after 30 months (Reeb 1969) — [Avni & Luria 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1359432X.2025.2457780). *These two figures for the interview conflict (0.39 vs 0.32).*
- **Retention in combat units:** An IDF English-language page quotes an officer saying the Quality Index helps predict retention in combat units. This is an official statement, not a study. — [IDF, "The Path to Becoming an IDF Soldier"](https://www.idf.il/en/mini-sites/our-soldiers/the-path-to-becoming-an-idf-soldier/) (page content via search snippet; the direct fetch returned empty)
- **Attrition from an IDF combat training course (Moran et al., 2011):** In 120 men aged about 18.7, an 8-month combat course attrition model predicted **69.8% of attrition** from medical health, entry physical fitness and psychological factors. The strongest correlates were **perceived fit, self-efficacy and self-confidence**, not cognitive ability. — summarized in [Coombs, Virginia Tech dissertation](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/753d80c9-f851-42c7-9a67-3c4560323988/content) (secondary summary of Moran DS et al., 2011, "Prediction Model for Attrition from a Combat Unit Training Program")
- **PTSD (combat), 2025–2026:** In IDF infantry over 3 years of service, lower IQ (Dapar) predicted steeper increases in PTSD symptoms, partly mediated by combat exposure (Zalmenson et al. 2025). In N=192 reservists, Dapar >50 (n=126) vs ≤50 (n=66) showed a Time×IQ interaction of F=3.27, p=.027, η²p=.017 (small). The groups differed at T3, but there was **no difference during the war, T4** (F=0.001, p=.97). The authors frame this as the "erosion of the protective role of intelligence" under extreme stress. — [Zalmenson et al. 2026](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/development-and-psychopathology/article/when-resilience-breaks-down-erosion-of-the-protective-role-of-intelligence-in-ptsd-under-extreme-combat-stress/094B9101FA5992A5F74D7A95F2916FB1)
- **Honesty (behavioural field experiment):** In 427 soldiers, a higher Kaba predicted *more honest* die-roll reports, with about **0.13 lower reported die outcome per Kaba point**. The result replicated with 156 civilians, in whom two cognitive-ability measures predicted honesty. — [Ruffle & Tobol, IZA DP 9860 (2016), later published as "Clever enough to tell the truth"](https://cdn2.psychologytoday.com/assets/dp9860.pdf)
- **Psychiatric epidemiology:** The Israeli Draft Board cognitive score is widely used as a premorbid-IQ measure in historical-prospective cohort studies (Rabinowitz et al. 2000; Weiser et al. 2007, 2017; Reichenberg's group), which cite Gal (1986) for validity — [Rotstein et al. 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/capturing-adolescents-in-need-of-psychiatric-care-with-psychopathological-symptoms-a-populationbased-cohort-study/8DD8499890537735E32DCCD6E494A0ED); [Zalmenson et al. 2026](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/development-and-psychopathology/article/when-resilience-breaks-down-erosion-of-the-protective-role-of-intelligence-in-ptsd-under-extreme-combat-stress/094B9101FA5992A5F74D7A95F2916FB1)
- **Data access:** A Max Planck demographic working paper notes that Israel conscripts both men and women, but "institutional barriers may prevent researchers from widely using these data" — [MPIDR WP-2017-020](https://www.demogr.mpg.de/papers/working/wp-2017-020.pdf) (via search snippet)
- **Israeli civilian analogue (NITE psychometric, not Dapar):** Controlling for gender, language sector, parental education, year and months worked, **+1 SD on the psychometric total ≈ +19% annual wage at age 35**, +1 SD quantitative ≈ +22%, and +1 SD Bagrut (with bonuses) ≈ +16%. Adding institution type and industry reduces these to about 7% (quantitative) and 5% (Bagrut). Data from CBS. — [NITE RR-21-01, Oren, Rapp & Maagan, Feb 2021](https://www.nite.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/RR-21-01-2.pdf)
- **Conscription-test analogues abroad (Sweden):** Wage returns of 11–13% per SD of cognitive skill measured at the draft — [Edin et al., IZA DP 10914](https://docs.iza.org/dp10914.pdf). About 21% higher labor income per SD, with a larger effect on capital income — [Bastani et al., "The Capital Advantage", Oct 2025 version (Swedish enlistment data)](https://www.spencerbastani.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Ability_Returns_Labor_Capital_Market_Oct_2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- The best-documented Dapar validity, about .40 against rank or general performance, is roughly what meta-analyses would predict for a g measure against training and advancement criteria (see Q7). It is **uncorrected or of unknown correction status**, and most of it is **40–60 years old**.
- Several recent studies agree that Dapar is a weak predictor of who *completes* combat or special-forces training. Attrition there is driven by physical fitness, self-efficacy and perceived fit (Moran 2011). This is consistent with the international pattern: cognitive ability predicts training *performance* better than training *attrition*.
- The PTSD findings suggest that g is a resource for coping under ordinary stress but does not protect under extreme, prolonged exposure. Dapar is therefore not a proxy for resilience.
- Rank at discharge is contaminated by the Kaba itself, because the Dapar gates officer-course eligibility (criterion contamination). The .41 figure likely *overstates* the Dapar's independent predictive power.

### Gaps
- **No accessible published coefficients** for Dapar or Kaba against: Bahad 1 completion or grades, the IAF pilot course, squad-commander courses, performance ratings, or first-term attrition across the whole conscript population. Such data surely exist in ממד"ה (IDF Behavioral Sciences) archives but are classified or unpublished.
- **No economics paper found** that links Dapar or Kaba to adult wages or education, despite targeted searches. This includes Ganzach and Lavy: Ganzach's intelligence-and-income work uses U.S. NLSY data, not IDF data. Swedish and Norwegian conscription data dominate this literature.
- The authors and year of the "Differential test validity for ethnic groups in the Israel army" article could not be retrieved; ResearchGate returned 403.
- I found no published studies by Reuven Gal, Ben-Shakhar, Kluger or Zeidner reporting new Dapar validity numbers. Gal (1986) is the cited source throughout, but its text was not accessible.

## 4. What the Dapar does NOT measure, and where the IDF assesses it instead

### Takeaway
The Dapar is purely cognitive. Personality, motivation, integrity, resilience, leadership, teamwork, physical ability and specific aptitudes are outside it. Historically the IDF covered some of these through the **structured personal interview (צד"כ, 33% of Kaba for men)**, education/Hebrew scores, consistency-check items, and later **gibushim/assessment centres** and specialized tests. The **2021 reform** explicitly added resilience, persistence, working memory, spatial orientation, fine motor skills, teamwork and leadership indicators to a new "fit score" (ציון התאמה). In practice the Dapar has remained in use as of 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Kaba composition (men):** Dapar 50% + personal interview (צד"כ) 33% + education score (שנו"ל) 17%. **New immigrants:** Dapar 60% + education 40%. **Women:** formerly 60/40 without an interview; since November 2017 women take the same interview (score called צמ"א). Kaba scale 41–56. Bands: 52–56 = elite units/officer corps *if Dapar ≥50*; 48–51 = command roles; 44–47 = regular service. — [Hebrew Wikipedia: מדד קב"א](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%93_%D7%A7%D7%91%22%D7%90). The 50/33/17 and 60/40 weights are confirmed by [Ruffle & Tobol 2016](https://cdn2.psychologytoday.com/assets/dp9860.pdf). They describe the 17% component as educational background (school, absences, discipline) and achievements (grades, clubs, distinctions).
- **The interview (צד"כ):** It is a structured interview conducted by trained young (mostly female) interviewers with a Kaba ≥54, assessing personality and behavioral traits. The protocol was "developed in large part" by **Daniel Kahneman** and "remains largely intact to this day". — [Ruffle & Tobol 2016](https://cdn2.psychologytoday.com/assets/dp9860.pdf); [Rotstein et al. 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/capturing-adolescents-in-need-of-psychiatric-care-with-psychopathological-symptoms-a-populationbased-cohort-study/8DD8499890537735E32DCCD6E494A0ED). Kahneman (1955) had interviewers rate six traits (e.g., "masculine pride", aggressiveness) and then give a final intuitive rating. The structured method was "much better" than the previous unstructured interview, and the closing intuitive judgment was "just as valid as the average of the six ratings". — [Alliance for Decision Education, "Deciding, Fast and Slow"](https://alliancefordecisioneducation.org/?p=8878); [Jason Zweig archive interview](https://jasonzweig.com/from-the-archives-daniel-kahneman/) (secondary; no coefficient given). Interview scale reportedly 8–40 (secondary encyclopedia summary) — [Hamichlol: מדד קב"א](https://www.hamichlol.org.il/%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%93_%D7%A7%D7%91%22%D7%90). *Note: Hebrew Wikipedia expands צד"כ as "ציון דרך", which looks dubious, and I could not verify the expansion. Sources describe צד"כ as the **interview score**, not a separate "motivation questionnaire" as the research brief assumed.*
- **Honesty checks inside the psychotechnical exams:** "Numerous questions on the psycho-technical exams are designed to evaluate the soldier's honesty", for example by asking up to ten variations of the same question to test consistency. Interviewers cross-check answers (drugs, police record, school absences). — [Ruffle & Tobol 2016](https://cdn2.psychologytoday.com/assets/dp9860.pdf)
- **Separate integrity test in officer selection:** A military-oriented integrity test predicted future disciplinary infractions among **467 officer-school candidates (ρ = .26)**. It also distinguished candidates from soldiers in military prison, and was assessed alongside cognitive ability, personality and assessment-centre ratings. — [Fine, Goldenberg & Noam, 2016, *JOOP* 89:198–218](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/integrity-testing-and-the-prediction-of-counterproductive-behavio/)
- **Assessment centres (gibushim) for command roles:** A field study of **10,898 candidates** for military positions compared virtual with face-to-face assessment centres. Reliability was similar across most dimensions, but ratings were significantly higher in the virtual format. — [Avni & Luria 2025](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/virtual-assessment-centres-versus-face-to-face-assessment-centres/)
- **The 2020–2021 reform:**
  - In 2020 the IDF piloted an algorithmic "fit index" (מדד התאמה) scoring motivation, physical fitness, cognitive ability, teamwork and command potential — [Hebrew Wikipedia: מדד קב"א](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%93_%D7%A7%D7%91%22%D7%90).
  - Announced in August 2021, effective 1 October 2021: "new, expanded adaptation tests" replace the Dapar in the fit score, and the new "fit score" replaces Kaba for placement. Inputs include character traits, teamwork, leadership, social mobility, hobbies, grades and study tracks (with Education Ministry data). Later additions were planned for mental resilience, persistence, working memory, spatial orientation and fine motor skills.
  - First-stage testing time was cut from 7 to 4 hours. The IDF bought a bank of 1,000 new items for about 300,000 NIS. It moved toward more non-verbal "formal" items, claimed (from unnamed internal studies) to reduce bias. An algorithm compares candidates with soldiers who "completed good service". Full rollout was planned within two years.
  - Source for the 2021 details: [ynet, Yoav Zitun (Aug 2021)](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/r1c3czwlk); [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%92_%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%98%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%A8%D7%90%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99)
- **What happened after 2021:** In 2022 the recruitment site switched from numeric Kaba to fit levels (low / medium / high / very high). In September 2024 the Kaba number was restored for display and later removed again. — [Hebrew Wikipedia: מדד קב"א](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%93_%D7%A7%D7%91%22%D7%90). Test-prep and guidance sites in 2026 still treat the Dapar as a threshold, for example "Dapar 90" for the Shahakim technology track (test-prep source, unverified) — [programmers-academy.co.il](https://programmers-academy.co.il/articles/%D7%9E%D7%A1%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%9C-%D7%A9%D7%97%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%9D/). A test-prep site claims the Kaba number was officially discontinued and that the Dapar, Hebrew score and officer-fitness level are shown instead (unverified) — [8200academy.com](https://8200academy.com/%D7%9E%D7%94-%D7%96%D7%94-%D7%A7%D7%91%D7%90-%D7%94%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%95%D7%9F-%D7%A9%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%9D-%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%A2%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%95/)
- **Critique of what the system measures:** Sociologist **Zeev Lerer**, a former senior IDF behavioral-sciences researcher, wrote *הקוד האתני: קב"א, מזרחים, אשכנזים* (Van Leer / Hakibbutz Hameuchad, 2021), based on formerly classified documents. He argues that the Kaba measured social and cultural background rather than military traits such as resilience and motivation. In his account, the Dapar favoured academic-track graduates and the interview was subjective. He reports that ethnic score gaps narrowed or disappeared on retests taken after adjustment to the army, and that peripheral Mizrahi candidates remained disadvantaged after the 1980s. — [Hebrew Wikipedia: מדד קב"א](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%93_%D7%A7%D7%91%22%D7%90); [Kotar record of the book](https://kotar.cet.ac.il/KotarApp/Index/Book.aspx?nBookID=108705829)

### Inferences
- **Map of what the Dapar does not capture, and the IDF instrument that partly covers each (from the sources above):**

| Trait / ability | In Dapar? | Where the IDF assesses it (if anywhere) |
|---|---|---|
| Conscientiousness / Big Five | No | Interview (צד"כ); personality items in the newer battery; AC ratings |
| Motivation / service orientation | No | Interview; 2020 fit index; unit-specific gibushim |
| Integrity / CWB propensity | No (only consistency items) | Consistency items; interview cross-checks; dedicated integrity test in officer selection (Fine et al.) |
| Resilience / grit / persistence | No | 2021 battery (planned); gibushim stress components |
| Emotional intelligence / social skills | No | Interview; AC group exercises |
| Leadership / teamwork | No | Gibushim / assessment centres (Avni & Luria); 2021 fit score |
| Physical ability | No | Medical profile; physical tests in gibushim (decisive for attrition: Moran 2011) |
| Spatial / mechanical / fine-motor | Only incidentally (timed matrices) | 2021 battery (spatial orientation, fine motor); specialized unit tests |
| Working memory (as its own score) | Indirectly (Arithmetic, Instructions) | 2021 battery |
| Coding / technical aptitude | No | Unit-specific tests (8200/technology tracks); not documented in peer-reviewed sources |
| Creativity / divergent thinking | No | None documented |
| Practical / tacit intelligence | No | None documented (partly via AC simulations) |
| Learning potential / dynamic assessment | No (static test) | None documented for mass screening |
| Hebrew proficiency | Partly a precondition | Separate Hebrew score; historically a gate before the verbal subtest (Flynn 1998) |

- The reform shows that the IDF itself judged the Dapar/Kaba system **construct-deficient**: too narrow, verbally loaded and SES-sensitive. Its response was to supplement the Dapar rather than abandon it.

### Gaps
- No public technical documentation, reliability or validity data for the post-2021 "fit score" or the new resilience, working-memory, spatial and motor tests.
- No authoritative source on the current status (2025–2026) of Kaba vs. fit score, or on how the Dapar is weighted in the fit algorithm.
- Specialized unit tests (8200, Talpiot, technology tracks, the IAF pilot "gibbush") have no peer-reviewed validity data that I could find.
- "Mitam" (מיט"ם), named in the brief, could not be identified in sources.

## 5. Incremental validity: what non-cognitive measures add beyond the Dapar

### Takeaway
There is no Israeli study that reports ΔR² of non-cognitive measures over the Dapar. The indirect evidence points the same way: the interview's validity (.32–.39 for rank) is comparable to the Dapar's (≈.40), and integrity tests predict disciplinary outcomes (ρ = .26). Recent meta-analyses rank structured interviews *above* GMA, which implies substantial incremental value.

### Cited Findings
- **Interview vs. Dapar:** Interview validity r = 0.39 for rank after 30 months, against cognitive r > 0.41 for rank at discharge (both via Gal 1986) — [Rotstein et al. 2021](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/capturing-adolescents-in-need-of-psychiatric-care-with-psychopathological-symptoms-a-populationbased-cohort-study/8DD8499890537735E32DCCD6E494A0ED). Interview r = 0.32 (Reeb 1969) — [Avni & Luria 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1359432X.2025.2457780)
- **Integrity test:** ρ = .26 for future disciplinary infractions among N = 467 officer candidates, described by the journal summary as a "valid and incremental predictor of counterproductive behaviours in military settings" — [Fine, Goldenberg & Noam 2016](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/integrity-testing-and-the-prediction-of-counterproductive-behavio/)
- **Combat-course attrition:** Physical fitness, medical status and self-efficacy/perceived fit accounted for most of the predictable attrition (69.8% classification) — [Coombs dissertation summarizing Moran et al. 2011](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/753d80c9-f851-42c7-9a67-3c4560323988/content)
- **Meta-analytic benchmarks (Sackett et al. 2022):** Structured interview ρ = .42, integrity .31, contextualized conscientiousness .25, overall conscientiousness .21, assessment centres .29, against GMA .31. The Black–White d is .79 for GMA versus .23 for structured interviews and .10 for integrity, which matters for adverse impact. — [Sackett, Zhang, Berry & Lievens 2023, *IOP* focal article, Table 1](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/S175494262300024Xa.pdf/revisiting-the-design-of-selection-systems-in-light-of-new-findings-regarding-the-validity-of-widely-used-predictors.pdf)
- **Validity–diversity trade-off:** Sackett and colleagues use an updated meta-analytic matrix to revisit the role of GMA tests in the validity–diversity trade-off — [Berry, Lievens, Zhang & Sackett, "Insights from an updated personnel selection meta-analytic matrix"](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/insights-from-an-updated-personnel-selection-meta-analytic-matrix/)

### Inferences
- Integrity and conscientiousness measures correlate only weakly with g in general. Combining a structured interview (.42) with GMA (.31) therefore plausibly yields a multiple R in the .45–.50 range under Sackett-type estimates. The IDF's 50/33/17 Kaba weighting is broadly consistent with that logic. *This is an inference from meta-analytic patterns, not an IDF finding.*
- For **training-heavy roles** (pilots, technology units, intelligence analysts), GMA's relative weight should be higher. For **combat-course completion and counterproductive behaviour**, non-cognitive and physical predictors should carry more weight.

### Gaps
- No published Israeli hierarchical regressions (ΔR²) of interview, personality, integrity or AC scores over the Dapar, for any criterion.
- No published utility analysis of the 2021 fit score versus the old Kaba.

## 6. Flynn effect and norm drift in Israeli military testing

### Takeaway
IDF data are a classic Flynn-effect dataset. Between 1971 and 1984, Israeli conscripts gained about **0.5–0.6 IQ points per year on the Raven-type Matrices** (≈15–18 points per generation) and about **0.35 points per year on the Otis-type verbal test**. Since the Dapar is reported in relative, stanine-like units, it must be re-normed periodically, but **no public information on re-norming** exists. The Matrices were no longer given to all examinees after 1984.

### Cited Findings
- **Matrices gains (males):** 0.510 IQ points/year in 1971–1976 (total 2.55); 0.600/year in 1976–1984 (total 4.80), i.e. a projected 15.3 to 18.0 points per 30 years. Females in 1976–1984: 0.637/year (5.09 total). — [Flynn 1998, Table 3](http://www.iapsych.com/iqmr/fe/LinkedDocuments/flynn1988b.pdf)
- **Instructions (Otis-type verbal) gains in 1976–1984:** males 0.365/year (2.92 total; projected 10.96 per 30 years); females 0.352/year (2.82 total). Flynn notes that the larger nonverbal-to-verbal gain ratio (about 1.8:1 for females) "is also in line with international data". — [Flynn 1998](http://www.iapsych.com/iqmr/fe/LinkedDocuments/flynn1988b.pdf)
- **Raw means rose year by year:** male Matrices raw mean went from 20.16 (1971) to 22.69 (1984), with SD shrinking from 5.24 to 4.82. Male Instructions went from 12.04 to 12.97. — [Flynn 1998, Tables 1a/2a](http://www.iapsych.com/iqmr/fe/LinkedDocuments/flynn1988b.pdf)
- **Gender:** Best estimates of Israeli female IQ are 98.6 (Matrices), 100.8 (Instructions) and 99.7 overall, i.e. "gender differences small". Lynn disputed the female sampling. — [Flynn 1998](http://www.iapsych.com/iqmr/fe/LinkedDocuments/flynn1988b.pdf); [Cambridge abstract page](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-biosocial-science/article/israeli-military-iq-tests-gender-differences-small-iq-gains-large/D3A19616EF0824D54F7F7C2FACDE107E)
- **Test discontinuity:** "1984 was the last year in which all those assessed took the Matrices"; computerized records began in 1970 and 1980 data are missing — [Flynn 1998](http://www.iapsych.com/iqmr/fe/LinkedDocuments/flynn1988b.pdf)
- **Meta-analytic context:** The Flynn-effect meta-analysis includes the Israeli conscript series, about 26,000 per year in 1971–1984 — [Trahan et al., "The Flynn Effect: A Meta-analysis", PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4152423/) (via search snippet)

### Inferences
- With fixed raw-score norms, a gain of about 0.6 points per year would shift roughly **one Dapar step (7.5 IQ points) every 12–15 years**. The IDF presumably re-standardizes stanines against recent cohorts, which absorbs drift but makes Dapar levels **non-comparable across decades**. A "Dapar 70" in 1975 and in 2025 are not the same absolute ability.
- The "IQ > 135 at the 95th percentile" mapping (Q1) may be an artifact of comparing Israeli scores with older, obsolete IQ norms.
- Several Northern European countries (Norway, Denmark, Finland) show a stall or reversal of the Flynn effect since the 1990s. Whether Israel has followed is unknown.

### Gaps
- No published Israeli data after 1984 on secular trends in Dapar raw scores; no published re-norming dates or procedures.
- No equating study between the paper-and-pencil and computerized adaptive Dapar.
- I found no study by "Tuvia Tal" or others extending Flynn's Israeli series.

## 7. Meta-analytic background: Schmidt & Hunter (1998), Sackett et al. (2022), ASVAB/AFQT

### Takeaway
The benchmark for GMA validity against job performance fell from **.51** (Schmidt & Hunter 1998) to **.31** (Sackett et al. 2022), and 21st-century studies suggest an even lower **.23** (Griebe et al. 2022). The revision is contested. GMA remains the strongest predictor of **training/learning** outcomes, where military evidence (ASVAB/AFQT, Project A) shows "not much more than g". The Dapar's ≈.40 validity for military rank and performance sits within this range.

### Cited Findings
- **Side-by-side estimates, Schmidt & Hunter (1998) → Sackett et al. (2022):**
  - GMA .51 → **.31** (SD ρ .14; lower 80% credibility value .13)
  - Structured interview .51 → .42
  - Job knowledge .48 → .40
  - Work sample .54 → .33
  - Integrity .41 → .31
  - Assessment centres .37 → .29
  - Conscientiousness .31 → .21
  - Unstructured interview .38 → .19
  - Interests .10 → .24
  - Black–White d for GMA = .79
  — [Sackett et al. 2023, *Industrial and Organizational Psychology*, Table 1](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/S175494262300024Xa.pdf/revisiting-the-design-of-selection-systems-in-light-of-new-findings-regarding-the-validity-of-widely-used-predictors.pdf); GMA r=.31 and structured interview r=.42 are also reported in [SIOP *TIP*](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance)
- **Lower again for modern jobs:** Griebe et al. (2022) meta-analysed 113 21st-century studies: mean observed validity **.16**, corrected **.23**. This drops GMA from 5th to 12th rank. Hypothesized reasons: fewer manufacturing jobs, more teamwork, and broader performance criteria. — [Sackett et al. 2023](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/S175494262300024Xa.pdf/revisiting-the-design-of-selection-systems-in-light-of-new-findings-regarding-the-validity-of-widely-used-predictors.pdf)
- **Training remains GMA's strongest domain:** "In jobs where lengthy and expensive training investments are made, cognitive ability remains the best available predictor", but "the relationship between ability and training performance has not been revisited" in light of the range-restriction issues — [Sackett et al. 2023](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/S175494262300024Xa.pdf/revisiting-the-design-of-selection-systems-in-light-of-new-findings-regarding-the-validity-of-widely-used-predictors.pdf)
- **Contested revision:** Oh, Le & Roth (2023, *JAP*) challenged Sackett's assumptions about range restriction in concurrent studies, and Sackett et al. replied — [UMN record of the reply](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/correcting-for-range-restriction-in-meta-analysis-a-reply-to-oh-e/). Commentaries (2023, *IOP*), among them Cucina & Hayes and Kulikowski, include ["Rumors of general mental ability's demise are the next red herring"](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/FC0F79EC4FDF5BEEA5F934E9098E2756/S1754942623000378a.pdf/rumors-of-general-mental-abilitys-demise-are-the-next-red-herring.pdf) and ["It takes more than meta-analysis to kill cognitive ability"](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/1DE03F9C14DE2C88447F27C67AC63F68/S1754942623000366a.pdf/it-takes-more-than-meta-analysis-to-kill-cognitive-ability.pdf), which argue that the lower estimates stem from choices about range-restriction corrections and supervisory-rating criteria.
- **ASVAB / U.S. Air Force:** Ree & Earles (1991, *Personnel Psychology* 44:321–332), "Predicting training success: Not much more than g". In about **78,000 recruits across 89 technical schools**, general ability was the best predictor in all jobs, and a common equation was almost as predictive as job-specific ones. Specific abilities added about **.02 (navigator) and .08 (pilot)** incremental validity on the AFOQT, mostly from job-knowledge tests. Corrected criterion correlations average **around .60**. — [typeset.io copy of Ree & Earles](https://typeset.io/pdf/predicting-training-success-not-much-more-than-g-13wqmhrqvz.pdf); [DTIC ADA247728](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA247728.pdf); [DHRA Paper 10: Use of Standardized Aptitude Tests](https://www.dhra.mil/Portals/52/Documents/DACODAI/Paper_10-Use_of_Standardized_Aptitude_Tests.pdf) (figures via search summary of these DTIC/DoD documents)
- **AFQT/ASVAB composites** are valid predictors of final school grades, technical-school completion times, first-term attrition and job performance. However, the Electronics composite outperforms g for electronics jobs, so g-dominance is not uniform. — [DTIC reports (search summary)](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA222190.pdf)
- **Project A** (U.S. Army, 1980s) developed hands-on performance criteria that index *maximum* performance. Sackett et al. note that cognitive ability predicts maximum (can-do) performance better than typical (will-do) performance. — [Sackett et al. 2023](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/S175494262300024Xa.pdf/revisiting-the-design-of-selection-systems-in-light-of-new-findings-regarding-the-validity-of-widely-used-predictors.pdf)
- **Israeli NITE comparator:** psychometric exam reliability .95; predictive validity for GPA about .3–.5 — [NITE 2023](https://www.nite.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/rel_val_acc.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The Dapar is best viewed as an AFQT-like g screen.** It is well suited to predicting **trainability**, which is where GMA validity is most robust. It is weaker, and probably over-weighted historically, for predicting **typical performance, citizenship, counterproductive behaviour, resilience and combat-course completion**.
- If Sackett's revision holds, the old justification for giving GMA 50% of the Kaba weakens. Structured interviews (.42) and integrity (.31) match or exceed GMA (.31), with far smaller group differences (d .23 and .10 vs .79). This supports the IDF's 2021 move toward multi-trait fit scores, *provided* the new measures have documented reliability and validity, which has not been published.
- The ASVAB literature implies that adding narrow cognitive tests (spatial, mechanical) to the Dapar would add little for *general* placement. It may matter for specific technical and pilot roles, where the AFOQT increment was about .08.

### Gaps
- No published IDF meta-analysis or recent large-sample validation comparable to Project A.
- The training-validity estimates for GMA have not yet been re-estimated under Sackett-style corrections, as Sackett et al. themselves acknowledge.
