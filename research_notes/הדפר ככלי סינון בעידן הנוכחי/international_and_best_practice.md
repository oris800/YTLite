## 1. International systems: what other militaries measure, how, and with what safeguards

### Takeaway
All the large systems surveyed still put a general cognitive-ability test at the front of the process. The more advanced ones add four things around it:
- adaptive/IRT delivery (US CAT-ASVAB; Sweden's SEB-2025 built on "modern test theory")
- a separate non-cognitive screen (US TAPAS; Finnish P2 test; Norwegian officer-rated suitability)
- several ability scores used for classification, not just one pass/fail number (US line scores; UK GTI with role minimums; Singapore vocation matching)
- explicit, written retest and confirmation-testing rules

The US Future Soldier Preparatory Course shows the risk in the newest trend, "prep-to-qualify" programmes. When retest controls were loosened, an Inspector General could no longer verify the score gains.

### Cited Findings

**United States: ASVAB / AFQT / CAT-ASVAB / TAPAS / FSPC**
- The AFQT is a composite of the ASVAB's verbal and quantitative subtests. It is "the primary measure used during the enlistment process to determine applicants' Military Service eligibility and to report applicant and recruit quality." The ASVAB as a whole is also used to classify applicants into occupations — [DoD OIG, DODIG-2025-092 (May 2025), citing USD(P&R) Dec 2022 paper](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2025-05/DODIG-2025-092.pdf)
- AFQT categories (DoDI 1145.01, incl. Change 3 of 19 Sep 2024): I = 93–99th percentile; II = 65–92; IIIA = 50–64; IIIB = 31–49; IVA = 21–30; IVB = 16–20; IVC = 10–15; V = 1–9 ("markedly below average") — [DODIG-2025-092](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2025-05/DODIG-2025-092.pdf)
- Legal and policy quality limits:
  - 10 U.S.C. §520 caps Category IV enlistees at 4% per fiscal year.
  - A service that exceeds 10% Category IV must run a Future Servicemember Preparatory Course and report to Congress.
  - DoDI 1145.01 requires at least 60% of enlistees to be at AFQT ≥50 (Cat IIIA or above).
  - Source: [DODIG-2025-092](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2025-05/DODIG-2025-092.pdf)
- CAT-ASVAB uses Item Response Theory throughout:
  - The 3PL model selects items, and a sequential Bayesian procedure updates the ability estimate after each response.
  - The official site says adaptive selection "yields higher levels of test-score precision and shorter test lengths than the P&P-ASVAB."
  - The speeded subtests (Coding Speed, Numerical Operations) were given non-adaptively.
  - Source: [officialasvab.com (CAT-ASVAB / IRT pages)](https://www.officialasvab.com/?p=1396); [NLS documentation](https://nlsinfo.org/node/434)
- The National Academies found that CAT-ASVAB measured the same constructs as the paper version, with similar reliabilities and validities. The move to CAT also improved security: fewer paper administrations plus "variability in content that adaptive item selection provided reduced the risk of a sudden and serious test compromise" — [National Academies, ch. on ASVAB methods](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/19017/chapter/15)
- TAPAS (Tailored Adaptive Personality Assessment System):
  - Developed by the Army Research Institute as a forced-choice temperament measure targeting "motivational aspects of performance."
  - In operational Army use since 2009.
  - Validated against attrition, course grades and completion.
  - Source: [ARI report, DTIC ADA591889](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA591889.pdf)
- Adding TAPAS composites to the AFQT improves prediction of cumulative 24-month attrition over the AFQT alone (likelihood-ratio improvement in nested logistic models) — [PMC table, Military Psychology study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10013451/table/T0015). A 2024 study models attrition separately as medical/physical, misconduct and performance types to see whether AFQT and TAPAS predict them differently — [PMC10013530](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10013530)
- A 2020 DoD/Army pilot allowed up to 6,000 applicants a year with somewhat lower AFQT scores to enlist if they held a high-school diploma and scored in the top half on TAPAS, i.e., a compensatory trade-off of cognitive against non-cognitive scores. The expected lower attrition was stated as a projection, not a result — [Army.mil, "New entrance test to increase Soldier quality, reduce attrition" (2020)](https://www.army.mil/article/231249)
- **Future Soldier Preparatory Course (FSPC), launched July 2022.**
  - Design: its academic track (Academic Skills Development Program, ASDP) takes AFQT Cat IVA/IVB applicants and teaches arithmetic reasoning, reading comprehension and word knowledge for up to 90 days. Trainees retake the AFQT through the Armed Forces Classification Test (AFCT) at least every 21 days, up to 3 times. They go on to basic training once they reach Cat IIIB (≥31) — [DODIG-2025-092](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2025-05/DODIG-2025-092.pdf)
  - Army-reported results: an average AFQT gain of 17 points, and 95% of students rose at least one category within two attempts. These are Army self-reported pilot figures — [Army.mil (2022)](https://www.army.mil/article/262368/pilot_program_helps_thousands_of_future_soldiers). More than 8,800 people had been "given a path to serve" by Aug 2023 — [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/268868)
  - **DoD IG findings (May 2025):** the ASDP was "effective in increasing the enlistment of category IV personnel," but the Army "enacted less restrictive testing standards." Specifically:
    - (a) Enlistment testing requires a confirmation ASVAB when a score rises ≥20 points; the ASDP required none.
    - (b) Army policy limits retests to every 6 months, yet trainees retested after as few as 7 days.
    - (c) Until Feb 2024 only one AFCT form was used.
    - (d) Recycled trainees mixing with new ones created a risk of item sharing.
    - (e) Drill sergeants held study sessions while also proctoring the AFCT, a conflict of interest under AR 611-5.
    - (f) Retest scores were used to reclassify enlistees from Cat IV to III, which the IG judged contrary to 10 U.S.C. §520. Once these were removed, the Army was out of compliance with the Cat IV cap and the 60%-≥Cat IIIA rule.
    - (g) Many trainees had limited English and were allowed to keep retesting.
    - About 15,259 ASDP trainees enlisted FY2022–FY2024. Four of five recommendations remained unresolved after the Army non-concurred.
    - Source: [DODIG-2025-092](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2025-05/DODIG-2025-092.pdf)
  - No independent (e.g., RAND) evaluation of whether FSPC graduates' later attrition or performance matches that of peers who qualified directly turned up in my searches (see Gaps).

**United Kingdom**
- The British Army Recruit Battery (BARB) has been replaced by the Army Cognitive Test (ACT). The Army's own Q&A says the ACT "is essentially the same sort of thing as the old BARB just a bit more up to date" — [Army Jobs official Q&A](https://ask.jobs.army.mod.uk/discussions/what-is-the-differences-between-the-new-army-cognitive-test-and-the-old-british-army-recruitment-battery-barb)
- BARB/ACT outputs a single General Trainability Index (GTI), with role-specific minimum GTI scores. Format details (e.g., ~45 min) come only from commercial prep sites and are unverified — [WikiJob (commercial)](https://www.wikijob.co.uk/aptitude-tests/test-providers/barb-test). The Cambridge Psychometrics Centre carried out a review of BARB — [Psychometrics Centre](https://www.psychometrics.cam.ac.uk/node/618). Early DERA research describes BARB as one of the main selection tools for Army entry — [DTIC ADA364247](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA364247.pdf). BARB was originally built on rule-based item generation (Irvine and colleagues, 1990s) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**
- Officer selection (AOSB) runs in two stages, Briefing and then Main Board. It includes group discussion, a planning exercise, an interview and a short lecture — an assessment-centre model rather than a single test — [Army Jobs, officer assessment](https://jobs.army.mod.uk/how-to-join/army-assessment/officer/)

**Sweden**
- The Swedish Enlistment Battery (SEB, the "I-test") has existed in various versions since the 1940s. SEB-2025 was introduced in May 2025 for almost all selection by the Swedish Defence Conscription and Assessment Agency (Plikt- och prövningsverket):
  - Built by the Swedish Defence University (2017–2024) with "modern test theory."
  - Structure: general ability (G) plus Gf, Gc and Gv, measured by six subtests, two per broad ability, each with 3–4 parallel versions.
  - Also used for police education and other total-defence professions.
  - Planned follow-up studies (2025–2029) cover norms, construct validity and predictive validity. **No validity or fairness results were published as of the April 2026 page update.**
  - Source: [Swedish Defence University project page (updated 13 Apr 2026)](https://www.fhs.se/en/swedish-defence-university/research/our-researchers/research-projects/follow-up-of-the-swedish-enlistment-battery-seb.html)
- Sweden is experimenting with specialised aptitude tests: a 2022 cyber-aptitude test (CTFS) validation used 62 conscripts. It cites a US finding that a cyber test added ΔR ≈ .05 over the AFQT — [Frontiers in Psychology 2022](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.868311/full)
- Swedish conscription data are widely used in research. One example: cognitive scores of 49,000+ men tested at 18–20 (1969–70 cohort) predict later disability pension — [PMC3797835](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3797835). Economists note that "motivation for the military service is likely to affect performance on the test of cognitive skill," a threat to validity in a compulsory, low-stakes-for-some setting — [Lindqvist & Vestman, AEJ:Applied appendix](https://assets.aeaweb.org/asset-server/articles-attachments/aej/app/app/2009-0140_app.pdf)

**Norway / Denmark**
- Norway uses a two-step selection: online pre-screening, then an in-person session. In 3,276 conscripts it predicted officer-rated basic military performance for both sexes, but the model explained only "a small, but significant part of the variance." The best predictors were tested physical fitness (men) and officer-rated suitability (women); GMA was one predictor among several — [Køber et al., Military Psychology (FFI record)](https://www.ffi.no/publikasjoner/arkiv/predicting-basic-military-performance-for-conscripts-in-the-norwegian-armed-forces); [UiT Munin](https://munin.uit.no/handle/10037/12703)
- Norwegian conscription ability scores are reported in stanines (1–9) and have been widely used in registry research — [Frisch Centre paper](https://frisch.uio.no/publikasjoner/pdf/2017/Formatert/Childhood_Intelligence.pdf)
- The Danish draft-board test (Børge Prien's Prøve) has test–retest r = .77, and its correlation with educational level has held at ~.5 for two decades — [PubMed 21198650](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21198650/)

**Finland**
- In 2025 the Finnish Defence Forces revised the **P2 test**, the psychological test used to choose conscript leaders. The revision was based on FDF studies and surveys of what conscript leadership requires today. The final score now indicates suitability for leadership or for "demanding rank-and-file duties" and can inform non-leadership placement (Research Manager Petteri Simola) — [FDF release via PUBT (July 2025)](https://pubt.io/view/9CD8711F861B2A452DA51C15F714EBB958069310)
- FDF conscript personality-test data (cohorts of ~420,000 men) show stable cohort trends; younger cohorts score as "increasingly sociable" — [Aalto University](https://aalto.fi/en/news/younger-cohorts-of-conscripts-are-increasingly-sociable)

**Singapore**
- Pre-enlistees take a computer-based psychometric test of about 1.5 hours on the same day as their medical screening. It "assesses aptitude to determine the appropriate vocation." Scores feed into vocation assignment and selection for leadership courses, along with medical fitness, skills, and the vocation interests pre-enlistees declare in advance through the NS portal — [CMPB, Psychometric test](https://www.cmpb.gov.sg/before-ns/pre-enlistment-process/medical-screening-and-psychometric-test/psychometric-test/); [CMPB pre-enlistment process](https://www.cmpb.gov.sg/before-ns/pre-enlistment-process/)
- In April 2026 MINDEF replaced the single A–F PES medical grade with a refreshed Medical Classification System of three separate assessments, to "better deploy" servicemen. This is a parallel move from one global grade to multi-dimensional profiling — [MINDEF news release, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.mindef.gov.sg/news-and-events/latest-releases/13apr26-nr/); [Fact sheet](https://www.mindef.gov.sg/news-and-events/latest-releases/13apr26-fs/)

**Germany**
- The new military service law took effect on 1 Jan 2026:
  - All 18-year-olds receive a questionnaire on suitability and motivation (mandatory for men, voluntary for women).
  - Men born in 2008 or later face mandatory medical examination (Musterung), with binding nationwide Musterung planned from 1 July 2027 in 24 centres.
  - Compulsory service is only "on demand," by a further Bundestag decision.
  - Source: [Bundesregierung FAQ](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/antworten-zum-neuer-wehrdienst-2397476); [Auswärtiges Amt](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/wehrdienst-bundesfreiwilligendienst/2099932); [deutschland.de](https://www.deutschland.de/en/news/bundestag-approves-law-for-new-military-service)
- Germany is therefore rebuilding cohort-scale screening from scratch, starting with motivation and self-report and then medical examination. Details of the cognitive testing in the new Musterung were not found (Gaps).

**NATO**
- NATO STO TR-HFM-290, "Advances in Military Personnel Selection" (May 2022), covers online selection testing, standardised assessments, the "war for talent," and integrity in selection — [NATO STO TR-HFM-290 (full report link)](https://www.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Technical%20Reports/STO-TR-HFM-290/$$TR-HFM-290-ALL.pdf). Earlier reports: TR-034 *Officer Selection* (2001); TR-HFM-107 *Recruiting and Retention* (with a Netherlands recruitment/selection chapter); TR-HFM-171 on SOF selection — [STO search results listing](https://www.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Technical%20Reports/RTO-TR-HFM-107/$$TR-HFM-107-ALL.pdf). I could only verify the HFM-290 contents at table-of-contents level: the full PDF and the executive summary did not download (404/failed).

### Inferences
- **The common pattern** is a short or adaptive g-test for eligibility and broad trainability, plus classification scores, plus a separate non-cognitive or motivational channel, plus a human judgment component for leadership (UK AOSB, Finnish P2, Norway's officer ratings). This maps closely onto the Kaba (Dapar + interview + education/Hebrew). So the Dapar's structure is not out of line internationally. The questions are about its precision, its delivery technology, its single-score format and its retest/fairness governance.
- **Two systems are closest to Israel's situation.** Sweden (SEB-2025) shows what a 2025-era conscription g-test looks like: a hierarchical CHC model (g + Gf/Gc/Gv), 6 subtests, 3–4 parallel forms per subtest, and a funded 5-year validation programme. Singapore is the closest structural analogue to Israel: near-universal male conscription, a one-day pre-enlistment session that combines medical and psychometric testing, and vocation assignment using declared interests.
- **The FSPC case transfers directly to any Israeli plan for prep courses or retests ahead of the Dapar.** Prep and retesting can raise scores, but without confirmation testing, multiple forms, minimum intervals and independent proctoring, the gains cannot be told apart from item familiarity, and the score's meaning degrades. It also shows that retest results reported as "quality" can hide a real fall in standards.
- **Language matters.** The FSPC's trouble with limited-English trainees mirrors the Israeli issue of Hebrew proficiency in immigrant and Arabic-speaking populations. A cognitive test given in a weak language partly measures language.

## 2. Modern selection science: validity of GMA and other predictors, incremental validity, validity–diversity trade-off

### Takeaway
The 2022 re-analysis by Sackett, Zhang, Berry & Lievens cut the long-cited GMA validity from .51 (Schmidt & Hunter 1998) to about .31 for overall job performance. Structured interviews (.42), job knowledge (.40), biodata (.38) and work samples (.33) now rank at or above GMA. Composites of several methods are only slightly less valid without GMA, but they have much less adverse impact (Berry et al. 2024). The revision is disputed but now dominant. GMA keeps its strongest case for predicting **training/learning success** and for cheap off-the-shelf screening, which is exactly the military use case.

### Cited Findings
- **Sackett, Zhang, Berry & Lievens (2022)**, *Journal of Applied Psychology* 107(11), 2040–2068, doi:10.1037/apl0000994. Earlier meta-analyses "systematically overcorrected" for range restriction. Revised mean validities are .10–.20 lower, though the rank order mostly holds, and structured interviews are now the top-ranked procedure — [SMU repository record](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/6894); [SIOP TIP summary](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance)
- Revised operational validities (overall job performance):

  | Predictor | Revised validity (2022) | Change vs. earlier estimates |
  |---|---|---|
  | Structured interview | .42 (SD .19; 80% credibility interval .18–.66) | — |
  | Job knowledge tests | .40 | — |
  | Empirically keyed biodata | .38 | — |
  | Work samples | .33 | .21 lower than Schmidt & Hunter |
  | GMA | .31 | from .51 |
  | Assessment centres | .29 (≈.33 with managerial-rating reliability) | — |
  | Interests (as person–job fit) | .24 | from .10 |
  | Unstructured interviews | not retrieved | .19 lower |

  Sources: [SIOP TIP](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance); [Sackett et al. 2023 IOP focal article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/core-reader)
- **Griebe et al. (2022)**, using only 21st-century studies, estimated GMA validity at an observed .16 and corrected .23. That moves GMA from 5th to 12th place among predictors — [Sackett et al. 2023, IOP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/core-reader)
- Sackett et al. say GMA's role is "reduced... but not refuted." It remains useful for off-the-shelf screening, for **learning and training criteria**, and for maximum-performance targets. They recommend:
  - comparing predictors at the low end of the credibility interval, not just the mean
  - local validation studies
  - custom-designed systems
  - "when in doubt, don't correct" for range restriction
  - Source: [Sackett et al. 2023, IOP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/core-reader)
- **Composites:** across all 1- to 5-predictor composites, mean validity is .51 on prior values vs. .47 revised. For a six-predictor composite, setting the GMA weight to zero drops validity from .66 to .46 on prior values, but only from .61 to .56 on revised values. Subsets with and without ability have "virtually identical" mean validity. GMA has the largest White–Black mean difference. It had the highest regression weight under prior values but ranks only 4th under revised values — [Sackett et al. 2023, IOP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D/core-reader)
- **Berry, Lievens, Zhang & Sackett (2024)**, *JAP* 109(10), 1611–1634, doi:10.1037/apl0001203. The updated matrix covers biodata, GMA, conscientiousness, structured interviews, integrity and SJTs. "Excluding GMA tests generally has little to no effect on validity, but substantially decreases adverse impact." Reaching an adverse-impact ratio of .80 still costs some validity, but less than previously thought. The validity–diversity debate "should shift from the role of GMA tests to that of other selection methods" — [UMN Experts record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/insights-from-an-updated-personnel-selection-meta-analytic-matrix/); [SMU repository](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/7568)
- **Dissent:** a commentary argues that the conclusion of much lower operational validity rests on how studies were sorted into concurrent vs. predictive designs — [IOP commentary (Cambridge)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/5C25EFD82F603039E111FFAF299B1B0F/core-reader). Another IOP commentary argues it is "time to end the horse race" of ranking single predictors — [Cambridge IOP](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E0A71A70C4C860B324399076B774F46F/S1754942623000275a.pdf/interpreting-validity-evidence-it-is-time-to-end-the-horse-race.pdf)
- **Military-specific evidence:**
  - TAPAS adds to the AFQT in predicting attrition — [PMC10013451](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10013451/table/T0015)
  - In Norway, physical fitness and officer-rated suitability outpredict GMA for basic military performance, with modest total variance explained — [Køber et al.](https://www.ffi.no/publikasjoner/arkiv/predicting-basic-military-performance-for-conscripts-in-the-norwegian-armed-forces)
  - A cyber-specific test added ~.05 over the AFQT for cyber roles — [Frontiers 2022](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.868311/full)
- **Validity–diversity dilemma literature:**
  - Ployhart & Holtz (2008), "The diversity–validity dilemma: Strategies for reducing racioethnic and sex subgroup differences and adverse impact in selection," *Personnel Psychology* 61(1), 153–172. They review ~16 strategies. The most effective is using or adding predictors with smaller subgroup differences (non-cognitive measures, interviews, SJTs) and reducing irrelevant reading/verbal load. Score-adjustment methods are less defensible — [doi:10.1111/j.1744-6570.2008.00109.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2008.00109.x) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**
  - Hough, Oswald & Ployhart (2001), *International Journal of Selection and Assessment* 9(1–2), 152–194, on subgroup differences across predictor types, adverse impact and mitigation — [doi:10.1111/1468-2389.00171](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-2389.00171) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**
- **US Army Project A** (1980s–90s): cognitive composites best predicted "can-do" criteria (core technical and general soldiering proficiency), while temperament measures best predicted "will-do" criteria (effort/leadership, personal discipline, fitness/military bearing) — McHenry et al. (1990), *Personnel Psychology* 43(2), 335–354, [doi:10.1111/j.1744-6570.1990.tb01562.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1744-6570.1990.tb01562.x) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**. This is the scientific basis for later adding TAPAS.

### Inferences
- **For the Dapar:** a short g-test with validity around .30 for job performance, possibly higher for training success, is a defensible component, but not a sufficient sole gatekeeper. The current consensus favours composites that weight structured (not unstructured) interviews and job-related or biodata information heavily. In principle, the Kaba's structure (Dapar + interview + education/Hebrew) already does this. The key empirical questions are how the components are weighted and whether the interview is truly structured (anchored rating scales, trained interviewers, standard questions).
- **Range restriction is less of a problem for the IDF.** Because nearly the whole cohort is tested, the IDF can compute validity in an almost unrestricted applicant pool. It should publish operational validities with conservative corrections, following Sackett et al.'s recommendations.
- **Validity–diversity.** In Israel the relevant subgroups are Arab/Druze, Haredi, Ethiopian-origin, recent immigrants, and periphery vs. centre. The Berry et al. 2024 findings imply that reducing the Dapar's weight relative to other valid components could reduce adverse impact with little loss of validity. This would need to be tested locally.

- I did not retrieve the Berry et al. 2024 composite weights or numbers.
- No meta-analysis of validity specific to conscript military samples, under the 2022 correction logic, was found.

## 3. Measurement error: why "zero error" is impossible (SEM, false positives/negatives, base rates, Taylor–Russell, banding)

### Takeaway
Every test score contains error. With realistic reliability (.80–.90), a few percent to more than 10% of examinees fall on the wrong side of any single cut-off. The rate peaks when the cut is near the middle of the distribution. Among people whose true score is within half an SEM of the cut, roughly 30–40% are misclassified. How much a test improves selection depends jointly on validity, base rate and selection ratio (Taylor–Russell). When almost everyone is accepted, the gain from any test is small. The professional answer is to quantify and report error at the cut and to use bands, multiple measures or confirmatory retesting near thresholds. "Zero error" is not achievable.

### Cited Findings and standard formulas
- **Standard error of measurement:** SEM = SD_x × √(1 − r_xx). An approximate 95% band is X ± 1.96·SEM. The standard error of the difference between two scores (e.g., a retest) is SE_diff = SEM·√2. These are classical test theory identities. The AERA/APA/NCME *Standards* (2014), ch. 2 "Reliability/Precision and Errors of Measurement," require reporting of reliability/precision and of standard errors, including conditional SEMs where scores are used for decisions near cut scores — [Standards, open-access edition](https://www.testingstandards.net/open-access-files.html) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**
- **Numeric illustration** (my calculation from the formulas above):

  | Scale SD | Reliability | SEM | 95% band | SE of a retest difference |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 10 | .80 | 4.5 | ±8.8 | 6.3 |
  | 10 | .85 | 3.9 | ±7.6 | 5.5 |
  | 10 | .90 | 3.2 | ±6.2 | 4.5 |
  | 15 (IQ metric) | .80 | 6.7 | ±13.2 | — |
  | 15 (IQ metric) | .85 | 5.8 | ±11.4 | — |
  | 15 (IQ metric) | .90 | 4.7 | ±9.3 | — |

- **Misclassification at a cut-off.** Calculation: true score T and observed score X are bivariate normal with corr(T, X) = √r_xx, and the cut is set at the same percentile on both:

  | Reliability | Cut at 10th pct | Cut at 30th pct | Cut at 50th pct |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | .80 | 6.4% | 12.8% | 14.8% |
  | .85 | 5.5% | 11.0% | 12.7% |
  | .90 | 4.5% | 8.9% | 10.2% |
  | .95 | 3.1% | 6.3% | 7.2% |

  Cells are the total share of examinees misclassified, split equally between false rejections and false acceptances. Example: at reliability .85 with a cut at the 30th percentile, about 7.9% of truly-above-cut people are rejected. This is my calculation, not a published table; its logic follows standard classification-consistency analysis.
- **Near the cut:** if a person's true score is k SEMs above the cut, the probability their observed score falls below it is Φ(−k) (my calculation):

  | k (SEMs above cut) | 0.25 | 0.5 | 1 | 1.5 | 2 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | P(observed below cut) | 40% | 31% | 16% | 6.7% | 2.3% |

  So for people near a threshold, a single administration is close to a coin flip.
- **Taylor–Russell (1939)**: the proportion of selected people who succeed depends on validity, base rate (BR) and selection ratio (SR). Taylor, H. C., & Russell, J. T., *Journal of Applied Psychology* 23(5), 565–578 — [doi:10.1037/h0057079](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0057079) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**. My bivariate-normal computations reproduce the classic table (e.g., r = .50, BR = .50, SR = .30 → .74):

  | Base rate / selection ratio | r = 0 | r = .20 | r = .31 | r = .51 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | BR .50, SR .30 (strict selection) | .50 | .59 | .65 | .74 |
  | BR .50, SR .70 | .50 | .54 | .56 | .60 |
  | BR .80, SR .90 (near-universal intake) | .80 | .81 | .82 | .84 |

  When nearly everyone is accepted, even a valid test changes the success rate of those selected by only a few points.
- **Banding:** Cascio, Outtz, Zedeck & Goldstein (1991) proposed treating scores within a band of width ≈1.96·SE_diff as statistically equivalent, with secondary criteria used inside the band. *Human Performance* 4(4), 233–264 — [doi:10.1207/s15327043hup0404_1](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327043hup0404_1) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**. Example: at SD 10 and r = .85, SE_diff = 5.5, so the band width is ≈10.7 points.
- **The US enlistment testing programme uses a confirmation-test rule:** a ≥20-point AFQT gain triggers a mandatory confirmation ASVAB — [DODIG-2025-092](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2025-05/DODIG-2025-092.pdf). This is an operational way of handling measurement error and possible compromise for big score jumps.
- **CAT reduces error where it matters.** Adaptive IRT tests give "higher levels of test-score precision and shorter test lengths," and precision (conditional SEM) can be targeted at decision points — [officialasvab.com](https://www.officialasvab.com/?p=1396)

### Inferences
- **On a 10–90 scale in 10-point steps (9 levels, stanine-like),** each step is roughly 0.5 SD wide if the scale is approximately stanine-based (stanine SD ≈ 2 units, so 1 unit ≈ 0.5 SD). An SEM of ~0.35–0.45 SD therefore means examinees within one step of a Kaba-relevant threshold have a substantial chance (~15–40%) of landing on the other side because of error alone. This needs checking against the Dapar's actual reliability and score distribution, which other researchers cover.
- **For a near-universal conscription screen,** the test's value lies less in the "accept/reject" decision (high SR, small Taylor–Russell gain) and more in classification across many jobs. There the stakes are the many fine distinctions (e.g., a Kaba threshold for a given unit). That is where SEM and banding matter most.
- **"Zero error" is unattainable.** The defensible goals are: (1) publish SEM, ideally conditional SEM at key thresholds; (2) use a second measurement or other information for borderline cases; (3) give a structured, timely retest route with parallel forms and confirmation rules; and (4) monitor both false-negative indicators (people screened out who later succeed via appeal or other paths) and false-positive indicators (attrition, training failure).

### Gaps
- No public figure for the Dapar's reliability or SEM was available in my scope, so the illustrations are generic.
- I did not find published classification-consistency figures for any military conscription test (e.g., SEB, Singapore).

## 4. Professional standards: AERA/APA/NCME Standards, ITC guidelines, Messick, Kane, fairness (invariance, DIF, differential prediction)

### Takeaway
The professional benchmark is the 2014 *Standards*. It is built on three foundations (validity, reliability/precision, fairness), with an argument-based view of validation: what must be validated is a specific use of scores, not the test in the abstract. Consequences of use are part of the evaluation. Fairness is operationalised as measurement invariance/DIF (same construct and item functioning across groups) and differential prediction (same regression line across groups). ITC guidelines add requirements for test adaptation across languages, technology-based delivery and security. A high-stakes, whole-cohort, multilingual test like the Dapar would be expected to document all of these publicly, at least in a technical manual.

### Cited Findings
- **Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing (AERA, APA, NCME, 2014).**
  - Part I has foundation chapters on Validity, Reliability/Precision and Errors of Measurement, and Fairness in Testing.
  - Later chapters cover test design, scores/scales/norms/cut scores, administration and security, and documentation.
  - The 2014 edition is freely downloadable.
  - Source: [testingstandards.net open-access files](https://www.testingstandards.net/open-access-files.html) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**
  - I found no evidence of a newer edition published by Nov 2026 (see Gaps).
- **Messick's unified validity:** validity is "an overall evaluative judgment" of how well evidence and theory support the interpretations and actions based on test scores. It integrates content, substantive, structural, generalizability, external and **consequential** aspects. Messick (1995), *American Psychologist* 50(9), 741–749 — [doi:10.1037/0003-066X.50.9.741](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.50.9.741) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**
- **Kane's argument-based validation:** state an interpretation/use argument (scoring → generalization → extrapolation → decision), then test its weakest inferences. Kane (2013), *Journal of Educational Measurement* 50(1), 1–73 — [doi:10.1111/jedm.12000](https://doi.org/10.1111/jedm.12000) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**
- **ITC/ATP *Guidelines for Technology-Based Assessment* (Nov 2022):** best practice for designing, delivering, scoring and using digital assessments across eleven areas, including technology-enabled test security and translation/adaptation for global testing. Free on the ITC and ATP sites — [SIOP TIP article on the new guidelines](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/new-guidelines-for-technology-based-assessment/); [PDF copy, v2022-11-08](https://rd.mandela.ac.za/rcd/media/Store/documents/RecH/Guidelines-for-Technology-Based-Assessment-v20221108.pdf); [Learnosity summary](https://learnosity.com/edtech-blog/atps-new-best-practice-guidelines-for-technology-based-assessment-a-summary/)
- The ITC also publishes *Guidelines for Translating and Adapting Tests* (2nd ed.) — [Wikipedia: International Test Commission](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Test_Commission). The 2nd edition dates from 2017 (published in *International Journal of Testing*, 2018) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**. It requires evidence of construct, method and item equivalence (e.g., DIF analyses) across language versions.
- **EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)** classifies AI used in recruitment/selection and in education admission/assessment as **high-risk** (Annex III). Under the 2026 Digital Omnibus, obligations for stand-alone Annex III systems were deferred from 2 Aug 2026 to **2 Dec 2027**, and for AI embedded in Annex I products to 2 Aug 2028 — [Gibson Dunn (May 2026)](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-); [Orrick (July 2026)](https://www.orrick.com/ja-JP/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/eu-legislators-agree-to-delay-for-highrisk-ai-rules). The stated reason was the late availability of harmonised standards — [Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/digital-ai-omnibus-delays-key-deadlines-introduces-new-rules/). I could not confirm the Official Journal publication date directly. Note that AI systems used **exclusively for military, defence or national-security purposes are outside the AI Act's scope** (Art. 2(3)) — [EUR-Lex, Reg. 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**
- **Fairness concepts as used in practice:**
  - Measurement invariance and differential item functioning (DIF): do items and factors function the same across groups at equal ability?
  - Differential prediction (Cleary model): is the regression of the criterion on the score the same across groups?
  - Both appear in the *Standards*' fairness chapter — [testingstandards.net](https://www.testingstandards.net/open-access-files.html) **[bibliographic, not re-fetched]**
  - Applied example: Sweden's SEB-2025 validation plan covers construct and predictive validity and norm updates but does not mention fairness or DIF — [FHS project page](https://www.fhs.se/en/swedish-defence-university/research/our-researchers/research-projects/follow-up-of-the-swedish-enlistment-battery-seb.html)

### Inferences
- **A Kane-style validity argument for the Dapar would need evidence for each link:**
  - scoring (item quality, IRT fit)
  - generalization (reliability; parallel-form equivalence across language versions and administrations)
  - extrapolation (predictive validity for military training and service outcomes, not just civilian job performance)
  - decision (cut scores in the Kaba and their consequences)
  - The weakest links to probe are probably language equivalence and consequences: placement into combat vs. intelligence/technology tracks, and later civilian labour-market signalling of the Kaba/Dapar.
- **Consequential validity matters more in Israel than in most countries.** IDF placement strongly affects later education and employment networks, so Messick's consequential aspect deserves particular weight. Group differences in placements that arise from construct-irrelevant variance (language, test familiarity, coaching access) are a validity problem, not only a fairness problem.
- **The EU AI Act does not apply to the IDF,** both because Israel is not an EU member and because military uses are excluded. It is still the clearest current regulatory statement that algorithmic screening for employment or education is "high-risk." It requires risk management, data governance, logging, human oversight and transparency, which are good-practice benchmarks if the IDF adds AI/ML to the Kaba.

### Gaps
- Whether a revision of the 2014 *Standards* has been published or is in draft as of late 2026: not checked/found.
- The exact current numbering of specific Standards (e.g., on conditional SEMs at cut scores and on cut-score documentation): not verified this session.
- The ITC test security guidelines: their existence and edition date were not verified this session.

## 5. New approaches 2024–2026: CAT/IRT, automatic item generation, game-based assessment, AI/ML, dynamic assessment, multiple-hurdle vs. compensatory models, continuous assessment

### Takeaway
- **CAT/IRT** is mature and already operational at military scale (CAT-ASVAB). It brings shorter tests, equal or better precision, and better resistance to item exposure.
- **Automatic item generation (AIG)** is the main way to supply the large item banks that CAT and frequent retesting need. Transformer-based AIG is emerging but still needs empirical item calibration.
- **Game-based assessments** show moderate convergent validity with traditional cognitive tests (r ≈ .5) and better applicant reactions. Their predictive-validity evidence is thin.
- **AI/ML scoring** is regulated as high-risk in the EU, and its validity and bias evidence is limited.
- **Dynamic assessment (Feuerstein's LPAD, developed in Israel)** shows that some culturally disadvantaged groups narrow gaps after mediation. However, the evidence comes mostly from proponents, and even sympathetic reviewers say it should not be used deterministically for prediction.
- **Multi-dimensional profiling with continuous in-service assessment** is the direction systems are moving: the Finnish P2 revision, Singapore's multi-assessment medical classification, TAPAS.

### Cited Findings
- **CAT:** IRT-based adaptive tests give higher precision and shorter length (CAT-ASVAB) — [officialasvab.com](https://www.officialasvab.com/?p=1396). They measured the same constructs with similar reliability and validity, and reduced compromise risk — [National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/19017/chapter/15)
- **AIG:**
  - AIG can rapidly create parallel forms so that different test-takers see different items of equal difficulty. It is motivated partly by test security in unproctored online testing. A dissertation found temporal stability and predictive validity for a non-verbal AIG measure (n = 333) — [Louisiana Tech dissertation (2017)](https://digitalcommons.latech.edu/dissertations/71)
  - The Cambridge Psychometrics Centre studied AIG's effectiveness for building cognitive tests — [Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/e3451e47-a0d8-43f5-8966-7f2df5d907da/full)
  - A 2025 critical review in *Journal of Intelligence* covers automatic and transformer-based (LLM) item generation. It notes that CATs are more resistant to item disclosure than multistage (MST) or linear-on-the-fly (LOFT) designs, but all three "drastically increase item construction demands," which is what AIG is meant to offset — [J. Intelligence 13(8):102 (2025)](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/13/8/102)
- **Game-based assessment (GBA):**
  - Leutner et al. (2023): a game-based cognitive assessment showed convergent validity r ≈ .50 with traditional tests and test–retest r = .68, in 11,574 applied completions, with favourable test-taker experience — [Frontiers in Psychology 2022/23](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.942662/full); [PMC9891208](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9891208)
  - Ohlms, Melchers & Kanning (2023, *IJSA*) built a game-based cognitive measure and compared it with paper-and-pencil (n = 183) for construct validity and applicant reactions — [search-result extract only; the record URL was not verified, so check the IJSA 2023 citation before use]
  - A meta-analysis (Heidelberg; *J. Intelligence* 12(12):129, Dec 2024) found stronger links between game indicators and cognitive ability when ability was measured with multiple indicators, and no difference by medium. Most GBA research has addressed personality, not cognitive ability — [MDPI J. Intelligence](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/12/12/129/htm)
- **AI/ML:** high-risk classification in the EU, with Annex III obligations deferred to 2 Dec 2027 — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/ja-JP/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes). Sackett et al. specifically call for research into how much structured-interview validity varies "especially with digital and AI-based scoring" — [SIOP TIP](https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance)
- **Dynamic assessment / LPAD (Feuerstein; ICELP / Feuerstein Institute, Jerusalem):**
  - Tzuriel & Kaufman (1999, *Journal of Cross-Cultural Psychology*): after a short mediation phase, young Ethiopian-immigrant children closed gaps with Israeli-born peers on post-teaching and transfer tasks, which static tests miss — [Bar-Ilan University record](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/1096)
  - A Feuerstein-Institute project used the LPAD plus an interview to select Ethiopian-origin applicants for university. It concluded that dynamic assessment may select disadvantaged students better than standardized psychometric tests. This is proponent-led — [BIU record](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/10073); [Infonomics Society paper](https://infonomics-society.org/?p=10608)
  - The CoReL programme (1997–99) used the LPAD with 666 recently arrived Ethiopian-immigrant students in 15 schools — [search-result extract; the hosting source is uncertain (Scribd copy of "Mediated learning and cognitive modifiability" or Unipa PDF)](https://es.scribd.com/document/168880299/modulated-learning-and-cognitive-modifiability)
  - A 2005 special issue concluded that dynamic assessment is more valid for planning and following up educational interventions, but "should not be used deterministically to predict" — [Tijdschrift voor Orthopedagogiek special issue 2005 (PDF)](https://www.spill.uantwerpen.be/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/TSJP_DynamicAssessmentInclEduc_SpecIssue2005.pdf)
- **Multi-dimensional, compensatory and continuous models in practice:**
  - The US TAPAS pilot let high TAPAS scores compensate for slightly lower AFQT scores — [Army.mil 2020](https://www.army.mil/article/231249)
  - Finland's 2025 P2 revision produces a score that informs both leadership and demanding rank-and-file roles — [FDF via PUBT](https://pubt.io/view/9CD8711F861B2A452DA51C15F714EBB958069310)
  - Singapore replaced its single medical grade with three assessments (Apr 2026) — [MINDEF](https://www.mindef.gov.sg/news-and-events/latest-releases/13apr26-nr/)
  - Norway's two-step model adds officer ratings during service — [Køber et al.](https://www.ffi.no/publikasjoner/arkiv/predicting-basic-military-performance-for-conscripts-in-the-norwegian-armed-forces)

### Inferences
- **The most feasible technical upgrade for the Dapar** is CAT on a calibrated IRT bank, plus AIG to grow and refresh the bank, plus multiple parallel forms. Together these address precision near thresholds, test length, item exposure and coaching, and they have a precedent at military scale. The calibration effort for an IRT bank is a one-off investment. Sweden's SEB-2025 (3–4 parallel versions per subtest) is a lower-cost middle option.
- **Dynamic assessment** is impractical as a whole-cohort first screen: it is individual, long and clinician-dependent. It is promising as a second-stage tool for borderline or flagged cases: new immigrants, low-Hebrew examinees, and people from groups where static scores may underestimate learning potential. That use matches its validated purpose (intervention and placement planning) and its domestic origin.
- **GBA and AI/ML** are not yet ready to replace a validated g-test in a high-stakes conscription context. If piloted, they should carry EU-AI-Act-style safeguards: bias audits, explainability, human oversight and logging. They should also be validated against military criteria first.
- **Continuous assessment** (training performance, commander ratings, in-service retesting) can correct first-session misclassification. That reduces the stakes of a single Dapar session, the error mitigation recommended in section 3.

### Gaps
- No published predictive-validity evidence for GBAs against military training or performance outcomes was found.
- No independent (non-Feuerstein-affiliated) predictive-validity study of the LPAD for adult or military selection was found.
- No information was found on whether any military currently uses AIG or LLM-generated items operationally (BARB's origins in rule-based item generation are bibliographic and not re-verified; current ACT practice is unknown).

## 6. Coaching, test preparation and retesting: effect sizes and how test owners mitigate them

### Takeaway
Retesting alone raises cognitive-test scores by about a quarter of an SD (δ = .26), and further with more retests: about +0.56 SD from first to third administration. With coaching, the gain is about 0.70 SD, and with identical forms 0.46 SD vs. 0.24 for alternate forms. Gains on identical forms fade with longer intervals. Mitigations that test owners use or that the literature supports:
- alternate or parallel forms and large item banks (CAT/AIG)
- minimum retest intervals
- confirmation testing after large jumps
- independent proctoring
- free official prep material to level access

### Cited Findings
- **Hausknecht, Halpert, Di Paolo & Moriarty Gerrard (2007)**, *Journal of Applied Psychology* 92(2), 373–385. 50 studies, 107 samples, N = 134,436:
  - Sample-weighted d = .24; corrected δ = .26 from first to second test.
  - Second to third test: δ = .20 (k = 16).
  - First to third test: δ = .56 (k = 15).
  - In percentile terms, a candidate at the 50th percentile could expect to reach about the 60th on a second attempt and the 71st on a third.
  - Source: [Cornell eCommons author version](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/4fbba765-0d9c-4940-8492-66fecaca5227/content)
- Moderators in the same meta-analysis:
  - **Coaching:** δ = .70 with coaching vs. .24 without (non-overlapping CIs). Coaching contact time predicted gains (β = .26).
  - **Formal instruction:** not significant.
  - **Identical vs. alternate forms:** δ = .46 vs. .24.
  - **Interval:** for identical forms, longer intervals predicted smaller gains (β = −.51). The predicted interval for identical-form gains to fall to the alternate-form level was about 685 days.
  - **Operational vs. research contexts:** similar (.29 vs. .24).
  - 25–50% of applicants in organisational and educational settings are retested.
  - Source: [Hausknecht et al., eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/4fbba765-0d9c-4940-8492-66fecaca5227/content)
- In the same paper, Messick & Jungeblut (1981) found SAT score gains rose with roughly the logarithm of coaching time. The contact time needed for gains above 20–30 points (on the 800 scale) "rivaled that of full-time schooling" — [Hausknecht et al., eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/4fbba765-0d9c-4940-8492-66fecaca5227/content)
- The authors warn that if coaching teaches test-taking strategies rather than the construct, "the validity of the measure would be compromised." Evidence on how retesting affects validity was scarce: only three studies. One (Lievens et al. 2005) found different validity coefficients for first vs. retest scores — [Hausknecht et al.](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/4fbba765-0d9c-4940-8492-66fecaca5227/content)
- A later meta-analysis (Scharfen, Peters & Holling 2018) reports an overall retest effect of d ≈ 0.24 and describes the Hausknecht base differently (75 studies). Check against the original — [Scharfen et al. 2018 (Gwern copy)](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/iq/2018-scharfen.pdf); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13423-018-1461-6.pdf). Most retest studies in the 2007 meta-analysis were educational, not workplace (84%) — [Purdue-hosted paper](https://apps.it.purdue.edu/sites/Home/DirectoryApi/Files/55709385-b8f8-48c4-90c5-7bd9d641bbc3/Download)
- **Operational military case (US FSPC):** coaching plus rapid retesting produced Army-reported average AFQT gains of about 17 percentile points — [Army.mil](https://www.army.mil/article/262368/pilot_program_helps_thousands_of_future_soldiers). The IG found the safeguards removed: a single AFCT form until Feb 2024, retests after 7 days, no confirmation test for ≥20-point gains, and instructors proctoring. It concluded these conditions "limit the Army's capability to assess the ability of trainees" — [DODIG-2025-092](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2025-05/DODIG-2025-092.pdf)
- **Mitigation through test design:** CAT's item variability reduced compromise risk for the ASVAB — [National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/19017/chapter/15). AIG supplies parallel forms — [Louisiana Tech](https://digitalcommons.latech.edu/dissertations/71); [J. Intelligence 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/13/8/102). Sweden's SEB-2025 has 3–4 parallel versions per subtest — [FHS](https://www.fhs.se/en/swedish-defence-university/research/our-researchers/research-projects/follow-up-of-the-swedish-enlistment-battery-seb.html)
- **Mitigation through equal access:** the official ASVAB programme publishes sample items and explanations of the test free of charge on officialasvab.com — [officialasvab.com](https://www.officialasvab.com/?p=1396) (the site's general existence and purpose are confirmed; the specific practice-material pages were not opened this session).

### Inferences
- **Size of the effect in Israeli terms:** a ~0.25 SD practice effect equals about 4 IQ points (0.26 × 15 ≈ 3.9). A ~0.70 SD coaching effect equals about 10 IQ points. On a stanine-like 9-step scale (1 step ≈ 0.5 SD), coaching of that size could move a candidate one step or more. That matters if commercial Dapar preparation is unevenly available by income or region.
- **The equitable responses are:**
  - (a) Make familiarisation universal: free official practice items and an explanatory video in all languages the test is given in. This removes the familiarity component of the gain, which is the main part for uncoached retesters (δ ≈ .24).
  - (b) Use large item banks with CAT/AIG to blunt item-specific coaching.
  - (c) If retests are allowed, use alternate forms, a minimum interval, a cap on attempts, and a confirmation test for large gains.
  - (d) Treat the retest score per a declared policy (latest, highest, or average). Averaging reduces both error and luck.
- **The FSPC lesson** is that prep plus retest is not illegitimate in itself (formal instruction that raises true ability is welcome). But the institution must keep testing conditions at least as strict as for the initial test, or the scores lose their meaning.

### Gaps
- Kulik, Bangert-Drowns & Kulik (1984) and Becker (1990) on SAT/aptitude coaching were not retrieved this session. Commonly cited values (from memory, **unverified**): roughly 0.1–0.15 SD for the SAT and larger for other aptitude tests. Verify before citing.
- No Israeli-specific coaching-effect study on the Dapar was found within this scope (other researchers may cover it).
- No research was found on how the US, UK or Sweden handle commercial coaching markets for their conscription/enlistment tests beyond publishing official practice material.
